# Content Quality & E-E-A-T Audit — autnic.com (2026-09-10)

Scope: homepage; reviews (Clair Obscur, Onimusha, Dawnwalker); news (GTA VI fishing, KOTOR five-years, Onimusha critical-split); guides (KOTOR play-the-original, GTA VI stealing-cars); setup (Clair Obscur platforms); 1 game hub (Clair Obscur); reviews tag/index page; about page; all 3 author pages; fr/de counterpart of the Clair Obscur review. Word counts and titles/H1s cross-checked against `autnic.com-audit/crawl.json`. Source-of-truth for frontmatter claims: `src/content/articles/{en,fr,de}/*.md`, `src/content.config.ts`, `src/content/authors/*.md`.

## Score: 76/100
Strong, well-disclosed review methodology and genuinely useful guide/setup content carry the site; it's pulled down by one unfulfilled trust claim (correction log), thin/near-duplicate news coverage built on other outlets, and generic non-verifiable author credentials.

## What works
- Reviews (Clair Obscur, Onimusha, Dawnwalker: 1,700–1,938 words) disclose tested version, platform, playtime, purchase/code source, and patch state in structured frontmatter (`testedOn`, `playtime`, `reviewNotes`, `method`) that renders as a "How we tested" block plus a verdict-plate line — e.g. Onimusha: `method: "Played on PS5 from a review code Capcom supplied... We bought a Switch 2 copy at retail on launch day; it is not reflected in this score"` (`src/content/articles/en/onimusha-way-of-the-sword-review.md:32`).
- Reviews carry specific, checkable claims a synthesizer would be unlikely to invent verbatim: "I lost a merchant in the second week and spent the rest of the run buying from somebody worse" (Dawnwalker review), Break Issen boss mechanic description (Onimusha). These are first-hand-shaped signals even though they can't be independently verified from the page alone (no reviewer-captured screenshots — see finding below).
- The About page ("Who we are") states an explicit editorial charter: paid-for copies, no pay-for-coverage, ad/affiliate separation from editorial, `rel="sponsored nofollow"` on shop links, and a named scoring rubric (0–10, one decimal) — this is exactly the kind of trust-page content the Sept 2025 QRG rewards.
- fr/de are full, independently-written translations, not machine-thin stubs: the fr Clair Obscur review runs 1,787 words vs. 1,666 en / 1,613 de, with its own idiomatic phrasing, not a 1:1 transliteration (`src/content/articles/fr/clair-obscur-expedition-33-review.md`).
- The GTA VI carjacking guide, written pre-release, is transparently sourced and hedged rather than presented as hands-on: "Everything below is from pre-release footage and Rockstar's own statements — none of it is confirmed final" and closes with an explicit "What is still unknown" section (`src/content/articles/en/grand-theft-auto-vi-stealing-cars-guide.md`). This is the correct way to cover an unreleased game and avoids the fabricated-experience risk flagged in the prior audit.

## Findings

### 1. [High] The site's headline trust claim ("dated correction log") is not demonstrated anywhere sampled
**Evidence:** The About page states: "Every article carries its own dated correction log, in the 'Corrections' block at the end of the text. We never quietly edit a published piece." (https://autnic.com/about/). The schema supports this (`corrections: z.array(z.object({date, text})).default([])`, `src/content.config.ts:64`), but a repo-wide check found zero articles across en/fr/de with a populated `corrections:` field (`grep -rn "corrections:" src/content/articles/*/*.md` → no matches). The rendered Clair Obscur review page has no "Corrections" section — only a separate "Revised score" block for the score change itself.
**Fix:** Either (a) stop asserting a per-article corrections mechanism on the About page until at least one real correction exists to populate it, or (b) if the block only renders when non-empty, add a placeholder state ("No corrections yet on this piece") so the claimed mechanism is visibly present rather than invisible-because-empty. A trust claim nobody can see is functionally the same as a false one to a quality rater.
**How we'd know it failed:** A rater or AdSense reviewer opens any review page looking for the "Corrections" block promised on /about/ and finds nothing — exactly what happened during this audit.

### 2. [Medium] Reactive news pieces built around a single competing outlet add real but thin value
**Evidence:** `onimusha-way-of-the-sword-critical-split.md` (782 words) opens "Kotaku calls Way of the Sword a game at war with itself. We scored it 8.2." and structures the entire piece as a compare/contrast with one outlet's review. The lede says the same: "Kotaku calls Way of the Sword a game at war with itself... Read both and the disagreement turns out to be narrow, specific, and about one design decision." Similarly the Dawnwalker "first-hours" and FF7 Revelation-dated pieces are framed around what Kotaku/another outlet reported. The Onimusha piece does add original synthesis (the "weighting is genuinely arguable" argument is Autnic's own take, not Kotaku's), so this isn't pure scraping — but it's the thinnest, most aggregation-shaped content type on the site and the one most exposed to a "does this add value beyond the source" QRG test.
**Fix:** Keep the outlet citation (it's honest and the sourcing is transparent — a real strength versus the fictional-games era) but lead each of these with Autnic's own reporting fact or angle before the competitor citation, so the piece reads as originated-by-Autnic-with-context rather than reaction-to-Kotaku. 
**How we'd know it failed:** Strip the Kotaku/competitor quotes from the piece and see if a distinct, non-generic argument remains; on this sample it barely does (the piece's own thesis — "not two critics disagreeing about quality... about what fraction of twenty-five hours" — is the part worth keeping).

### 3. [Medium] No first-hand visual evidence — all article imagery is publisher press assets, not reviewer captures
**Evidence:** Every sampled review's hero image is captioned with a publisher/press credit, e.g. Clair Obscur: "Sandfall Interactive / Kepler Interactive · Éditeur — usage éditorial"; Onimusha and Dawnwalker covers follow the same pattern (`cover:` + `coverCaption:` in frontmatter, credited to the publisher, not to Autnic or the named reviewer). Nothing in the sampled reviews (own screenshots, video capture, platform UI in the reviewer's own save state) exists to visually corroborate the playtime/version claims in the text.
**Fix:** For at least the flagship long reviews, add 1–2 reviewer-captured screenshots (own save, own settings menu, own scoreboard/end-credits screen) alongside the press key art, captioned as such. This is the single highest-leverage experience signal missing given the site is already image-led.
**How we'd know it failed:** Every image credit on a review page still reads "[Publisher] · Éditeur — usage éditorial" with none attributed to the author or to Autnic's own capture.

### 4. [Low] Author credentials are asserted, not verifiable
**Evidence:** Sacha Vidal's bio: "Twelve years in the specialist press, four of them covering console releases day to day." (`src/content/authors/sacha-vidal.md:11`, and rendered identically on /about/ and /author/sacha-vidal/). No named prior outlet, no external profile link (LinkedIn, Muck Rack, a byline archive elsewhere), no photo — avatars are two-letter initials (SV/LM/NB) site-wide. Lina Morel ("Former QA tester") and Nour Benali have the same pattern: specific-sounding but unverifiable claims.
**Fix:** Add one external, checkable credential per author (a link to a prior byline, a Twitter/X or LinkedIn, or a real photo) — doesn't need to be elaborate, just falsifiable, which is the actual QRG bar for expertise signals.
**How we'd know it failed:** Search the named claim ("twelve years in the specialist press") and find nothing outside autnic.com that corroborates it.

### 5. [Info] Homepage H2 "Your ultimate gaming blog" is confirmed leftover template copy
**Evidence:** Present verbatim in the rendered homepage text extraction, immediately above the "Everything / Guides / News / Reviews / Setup" filter pills (line 83 of extracted text; per the orchestrator's own repo trace this is `class="pick-h"` in `src/views/Home.astro`, likely sourced from i18n strings). It contradicts the site's actual positioning (an independent review/guide/news publication, not a "blog") and is the kind of generic-AI-template tell the Sept 2025 QRG explicitly flags.
**Fix:** Replace with copy consistent with the rest of the homepage's editorial voice, e.g. a line naming what's below ("This week on Autnic") rather than a generic CMS-starter label. Keep it short — it's a section eyebrow, not a hero headline.
**How we'd know it failed:** The string "ultimate gaming blog" still appears anywhere in rendered HTML.

### 6. [Info] News word counts (610–930) sit below general blog-post floors but the format doesn't need them
Not a defect on its own — dated news items are a different page type than long-form blog posts, and QRG/word-count guidance treats minimums as topical-coverage floors, not targets. Flagging only because 9 of the 21 English articles (the largest single content type) are in this band, so if any are later expanded for SEO purposes, expand with reporting (a new fact, a follow-up check) rather than padding, to avoid diluting the aggregation-vs-original-value balance noted in Finding 2.

The risk has not "changed shape" into a new abuse pattern in the pages sampled — see Finding 3 for the residual, much smaller gap (no first-party visual proof).
