Onimusha: Way of the Sword launched today on four platforms at once — PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S,
Nintendo Switch 2, and PC through Steam and Epic. That is a normal spread. What is not normal is the
order the reviews came back in.

## The four versions and the scores

As of this morning, Metacritic has the game in generally favourable territory everywhere, with the
platform averages sitting like this:

- **Nintendo Switch 2 — 88**
- **PlayStation 5 — 85**
- **Xbox Series X — 83**
- **PC — 82**

OpenCritic has 94% of critics recommending the game overall. So every version is well reviewed, and
nobody is telling you to avoid one. But the handheld-class hardware is at the top of the list and the
platform with the highest technical ceiling is at the bottom, which is close to the reverse of what a
multiplatform action game normally produces.

## What a Metacritic spread actually measures

Before treating six points as a hardware verdict, it is worth being precise about what a per-platform
average is. It is not a controlled comparison. It is a different set of outlets, reviewing on
different schedules, with different house score distributions, writing about the same game.

A Switch 2 average is disproportionately made of outlets whose beat is Nintendo hardware, and the
question those outlets are implicitly answering is "is this good *on this machine*" — a question with
a genuinely impressive answer when a current RE Engine action game runs on Switch 2 at all. A PC
average is disproportionately made of outlets who benchmark, and whose reviews carry the cost of
whatever the launch build's settings menu and performance look like on a spread of configurations.

Both numbers are honest. They are not measuring the same thing, and subtracting one from the other
does not give you a hardware ranking.

## Why the PC number is the one to be careful with

An 82 for a PC version is comfortably good, and it is also the number most likely to move. PC review
scores at launch are the most sensitive to things that get patched: stutter, shader compilation,
settings that do not do what they say. If the game runs well on your machine, an 82 built partly on
first-week technical complaints has nothing to say to you.

The reverse is also true. If you have a mid-range PC and no patience for a launch-window troubleshoot,
the 82 is a real signal and the console versions exist precisely for you. Steam's refund window is the
right tool here: the game is around twenty hours long, so two hours is a genuinely useful sample of
the combat system without getting close to the refund limit.

The PC release is on both Steam (appid 2638890) and Epic. We have no basis for recommending one
storefront over the other.

## The case for Switch 2

Take the 88 seriously in one specific way: the reviewers who scored it highest were reviewing a game
that plays in your hands, and Way of the Sword is unusually well suited to that. It is about twenty
hours long, largely linear, and built out of short, self-contained encounters. That is a shape that
survives being played in twenty-minute pieces better than an open-world game does.

If you already own a Switch 2 and you were going to buy this at all, the portable version is not a
compromise version. That is the actual news in the 88.

## The case for PS5 or Xbox Series

These are the versions that will look best on a television, and the PS5's 85 is the second-highest
number on the board. If you have one and a Switch 2 and you do not care about portability, PS5 is the
defensible default.

The Xbox Series 83 sits between the PS5 and PC numbers and is drawn from a smaller pool of reviews
than either. We would not read anything into three points of difference from the PS5 average.

## What we have not verified

Our review was written from a PS5 code Capcom supplied ahead of launch. We bought a Switch 2 copy at
retail this morning and have not put meaningful hours into it. We have not played the Xbox Series or
PC versions.

So: everything above about the PS5 version is ours, and everything above about the other three is a
reading of other people's scores, presented as that. If we find a version difference that changes the
recommendation once we have played the Switch 2 build properly, this page gets updated and the review
score gets re-checked rather than left alone.
