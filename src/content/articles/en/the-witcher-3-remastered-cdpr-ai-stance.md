**CD Projekt** joint-CEO **Michał Nowakowski** was asked about artificial intelligence during the
company's latest earnings briefing, and gave an answer that is worth reading twice. AI is
"definitely becoming a thing in games development", he said — and the studio is still
"predominantly using people" to make its games. **GameSpot** reported the remarks on September 3,
citing PC Gamer's coverage of the briefing.

"AI, if anything, is a tool like many others that's helping in places that can maybe become more
helpful in the future," Nowakowski said, "but we're not planning to rely on AI making complete
games."

## The part he declined to say

Asked whether that holds for the industry generally, he did not take it.

"So whether this is true for the whole industry, I'm not the person to make those statements," he
said. "I think everybody has to make those judgments on their own."

That is a narrower statement than the headline version. It commits CD Projekt to a position and
explicitly refuses to commit anyone else — including, read strictly, CD Projekt at a later date.
Earnings briefings are where companies say the thing they are prepared to be held to, and this
was carefully bounded.

## Why an earnings call is where this happened

It matters that the question came from investors rather than press. Two years ago the pressure in
that room ran the other way — studios were asked what they were doing with AI, with the implied
answer being *more*. A CEO now volunteering that his team is mostly human is a sign the audience
for that answer has changed.

Nowakowski's framing splits the difference: AI as a tool used in places, no plan to generate
whole games. That is close to unfalsifiable as a commitment, and it is still more than most
publishers have put on a transcript.

## What this has to do with the remaster

More than it looks. A remaster is the part of the catalogue where automated tooling is most
tempting and least visible — upscaled textures, generated detail, machine-assisted asset passes
across a world built in 2015. When a studio says its next mainline game is made predominantly by
people, the reasonable follow-up is what standard applies to the back catalogue, and nobody put
that question to Nowakowski.

*The Witcher 4* is set for **2028**, opening a new trilogy, with *Cyberpunk 2* and a multiplayer
*Witcher* game also in development.
