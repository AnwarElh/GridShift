The question a six-year-old live-service game has to answer is not "is it good" but "is it still
worth starting". Genshin's answer is unusual, and it has almost nothing to do with the gacha.

## What it does that nothing else does

Every element in Genshin is a verb, and the verbs combine. Water plus cold is frozen; lightning plus
water spreads; fire plus wind carries. Six years of imitators have copied the character-collection
shell and left this part alone, because it is hard: it requires every enemy, every surface and every
puzzle to respect the same rules. The result is a combat system where a team is a sentence rather
than a stat sheet, and where a player who understands reactions clears content that a player with
better characters cannot.

The world is the other half. Teyvat is hand-authored to a degree that is genuinely rare — no
procedural terrain, no repeated outpost layouts. Nod-Krai, the current destination, is an autonomous
region of southern Snezhnaya built on Slavic and northern European motifs, and it is the first real
look at the Cryo nation the game has been circling since launch. Six years in, HoYoverse is still
raising its own bar on region design. That is not nothing.

## What it costs you

Original Resin regenerates at one unit every eight minutes and caps at 200. Everything that makes a
character stronger — artifacts, talent books, weapon materials, boss drops — spends it. Play for six
hours and the last five are exploration and story, because the progression ran out in the first
forty minutes.

Defenders call this respectful of your time. It is more honest to call it what it is: a rate limiter
on the only loop that has a spending pressure attached. You cannot grind past a bad artifact week.
You wait, or you buy Fragile Resin.

And then the banner. Soft pity begins around pull 74, hard pity at 90, and the first 5-star is a
coin flip against the permanent pool. Lose it and the guarantee makes the next one certain — so the
worst case for a specific character is 180 pulls. That is the arithmetic, stated plainly, and it has
not moved in six years.

## Artifacts are the real grievance

Ask a long-term player what they would change and almost nobody says the banner. They say artifacts.
Five slots, each with a main stat and four substats rolled at random, upgraded in increments that are
also random. A piece can be correct in set, correct in slot, correct in main stat and still be
worthless four times over. The resin cost of finding out is the same either way.

## Who should start now

Nod-Krai is a reasonable entry point precisely because a new region is a map, and a map does not
require you to remember a plot. If you want a single-player action RPG with the best traversal and
the best combat in its category and you are content to treat the daily loop as optional, this is
still the game. If you want to build a specific character on a specific timeline, price it first.

## Where the score sits

8.2 is not a compromise between "great world" and "bad monetisation". It is the score of a game whose
best hour is better than almost anything in the genre and whose worst hour is a countdown timer. Both
are the design. Neither is going away.
