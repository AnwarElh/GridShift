Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic – Remake was announced on 9 September 2021. Five years later
there is no release date, no release window, no store page, and not one second of gameplay in
public. This is what a game looks like when it has been in development long enough for the studio
making it to change.

## The reveal, and the stop

Aspyr Media revealed the remake in September 2021. Aspyr's reputation to that point was built on
ports and conversions rather than ground-up production, and the announcement was the studio's
largest project by a wide margin.

In July 2022 development was halted. What has never been publicly detailed is the state the project
was in when that happened — how much existed, how much was usable, or what the specific trigger
was. Saber Interactive subsequently took the project over and, by the account of everyone who has
spoken about it since, restarted it from scratch. Roughly three years of that rebuild have now
elapsed.

## Embracer kept saying it would ship

Through the halt and the handover, Embracer Group's public line held: in May 2023 the company was
still insisting the remake would eventually be released. That was three years and one corporate
restructuring ago, and it has not been retracted.

An owner saying a game will ship is not a schedule. It is a statement that the project has not been
cancelled, which — for a game that stopped once already — is not nothing, and is also not much.

## What Saber has actually said

In March 2026, Saber chief creative officer Tim Willits was asked directly and answered: **"Yes, it
is still in development. That's all I can say."** Separately, Saber's CEO said the game is **"alive
and well."**

Read those two sentences carefully, because they are close to the entire official record for 2026.
Neither describes a feature. Neither names a platform. Neither implies a stage of production. They
confirm the project has a pulse and decline every follow-up question, which is what a studio says
when a marketing plan exists but is not theirs to trigger.

## The 2028 remark

In July 2026 Saber's new studio head gestured at a release year, and a statement referenced the
remake "hopefully" being in the studio's lineup for **2028**. That word is doing a lot of work.
"Hopefully in the 2028 lineup" is an aspiration inside a slate, not a date, and it is the furthest
forward anything official has gone.

If it is accurate, the remake would arrive roughly seven years after its own announcement, from a
studio that started over in the middle.

## What is not known

The honest inventory is short and worth stating plainly, because a lot of coverage rounds it off.

- **No release date. No release window.** Nothing has been announced.
- **No platforms confirmed** by Saber or Lucasfilm Games.
- **No gameplay.** Not a trailer, not a screenshot, not a captured frame.
- **No store page** anywhere.
- **No confirmed showcase appearance.** Nothing has been scheduled in public.
- **No description of scope.** Nobody outside the project has said what "remake" means here —
  whether the 2003 systems are being reproduced, adapted, or replaced.

Separately, an insider account claims a re-reveal is planned before the end of 2026 and that
development is going smoothly. That is a rumour, it is unverified, and we cover it separately as
one.

## Why we are writing this at all

Because the alternative is what usually fills the gap: reheated 2021 announcement art, speculation
written in the present tense, and feature lists assembled from a game that came out in 2003. A
five-year silence is a real story. Inventing detail to decorate it is not.

Until Saber shows something, the only Knights of the Old Republic anyone can play is the one from
2003 — which is still on sale, still excellent, and needs a little setup work to sit properly on a
2026 display. Our guide to that is in the guides section.
