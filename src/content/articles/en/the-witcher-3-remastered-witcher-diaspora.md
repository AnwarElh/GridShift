In a twenty-six day window this month, three separate companies are shipping or building work
descended from a single 2015 role-playing game. All three are staffed, at leadership level, by people
who made that game. None of them is CD PROJEKT RED alone.

That is an unusual shape for an industry story, and it is worth laying out plainly, because the
temptation is to turn it into a rivalry and it is not one.

## The three

**Rebel Wolves** released **The Blood of Dawnwalker** on **September 3**. The studio was founded in
February 2022 by **Konrad Tomaszkiewicz** — the director of *The Witcher 3* and head of production on
*Cyberpunk 2077* — after seventeen years at CD PROJEKT. Its leadership is a roll call from the same
credits: creative director **Mateusz Tomaszkiewicz**, design director **Daniel Sadowski**, narrative
director **Jakub Szamałek**, art director **Bartłomiej Gaweł**. The team has grown from around twenty
people to roughly a hundred and sixty.

**Yigsoft** is co-developing **The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt — Remastered**, out **September 29**. CD
PROJEKT RED describes it as "a team with a proven track record working on The Witcher 3 already."

**Fool's Theory** is co-developing **Songs of the Past**, the new *Witcher 3* expansion due in 2027.
CDPR describes its leadership as "made up of *Witcher 3* veterans."

So: the people who made *The Witcher 3* are, this month, simultaneously competing with it, remastering
it, and expanding it.

## What this is not

It is not a feud. Neither company has said a word about the other. Rebel Wolves has never framed
*Dawnwalker* against *The Witcher 3*, and CD PROJEKT RED has never mentioned Rebel Wolves. The
September 3 and September 29 dates were set by different companies for different reasons, and a
twenty-six day gap between a new IP and a free remaster is not a collision in any commercial sense —
they are not competing for the same purchase, because one of them is not a purchase.

It is also not a story about a studio losing its people. CD PROJEKT RED still made *Phantom Liberty*,
still has a *Witcher* trilogy in development, and is co-ordinating two co-developed projects on this
game alone. Departures at this scale are normal for a company that has been shipping for twenty-four
years.

## What it actually shows

Two things, and both are about how expertise behaves when it leaves a building.

**First: it clusters.** Tomaszkiewicz did not leave to join a publisher. He left, took an extended
break, and founded a studio that reassembled a specific slice of the *Witcher 3* leadership — the
director, the creative director, the design director, the narrative director, the art director.
Fool's Theory did the same thing with a different slice. Yigsoft is a third variation. The knowledge
did not diffuse across the industry; it reformed into smaller units that kept working on the same
kind of thing.

**Second: the smaller units want to be smaller.** Tomaszkiewicz has been explicit that after
seventeen years he wanted smaller projects with smaller teams, having seen what large-scale
development at a five-hundred-person company costs. *The Blood of Dawnwalker* is the argument in
practice — a fifty-to-seventy hour RPG built around one tight structural idea, made by a hundred and
sixty people, which reviewers received as focused rather than thin. *Video Games Chronicle*'s review
headline was that "*Witcher 3* veterans prove a focused RPG beats a bloated one."

Whether that is a durable model or a first-game advantage is the open question. Studios get bigger.
Second games are harder. The interesting thing about this particular September is that both theories
are being tested at once, by people who used to sit near each other.

## The thing that makes it legible

There is a reason all of this is visible now rather than diffuse across five years, and it is
mundane: *The Witcher 3* is eleven years old and the industry has arrived at the point in the cycle
where its remaster, its new expansion, and the first major original game from its alumni all become
commercially sensible in the same quarter.

That is not a narrative. It is a schedule. But it does produce a genuinely rare view — the same
creative lineage, at the same moment, in three different corporate configurations, with the results
of each one on sale or dated.

For anyone deciding what to play: *Dawnwalker* is out and reviewed. *Remastered* is free on the 29th
if you already own the game. *Songs of the Past* is 2027 and paid. The lineage is a good story; it is
not a purchasing recommendation, and the three products have very little to do with each other.
