*The Blood of Dawnwalker* has been out for days, and the most popular thing PC players have done
with it so far is turn off its central mechanic. The **Better Story Timer** mod, uploaded to
Nexus Mods on Wednesday, gives players far more time for everything and can remove some of the
time restrictions outright. **Kotaku** reported on September 4 that it had passed **36,000
downloads**, adding more than 5,000 on that day alone.

## What the mod actually removes

Rebel Wolves built *Dawnwalker* around thirty in-game days and nights, each split into eight
segments, with only plot-relevant actions — quests and levelling — spending them. Exploration is
free. There is no real-time clock. An hourglass shows the cost of an activity before you commit
to it. And the game deliberately contains more time-consuming quests than there are segments to
spend, so no single run can see everything.

That last part is the design. The scarcity is the point: it is what turns a quest list into a set
of choices, and it is the thing the studio talked about most in the year before launch.

Better Story Timer takes the scarcity out. The other mods climbing alongside it do the same to
the game's other limits — bigger carry weight, extra loot, faster levelling, more talent points.
Kotaku's framing is that a chunk of the PC audience simply has no patience for friction of this
kind, and that if the settings menu will not let them tune it out, the mod page will.

## The number needs context, though

36,000 downloads is a real figure and a loud one, but it is not a referendum. Kotaku notes that
SteamDB showed **over 50,000 people playing concurrently** at the time of writing — and
concurrent players are a slice of total buyers, which is itself a slice larger again once
consoles are counted. This is not eighty percent of the PC base rejecting the design. It is a
visible, motivated minority doing extra work to get the version of the game they wanted.

That is still worth noticing. Nobody installs a mod by accident.

## Where it leaves the design

There is no damage here in the usual sense. *Dawnwalker* is single-player, so a modded run costs
no one else anything, and PC modding has always been the place where players finish arguing with
a designer in their own save file.

What it does expose is a gap between how the thirty-day structure reads in a preview and how it
feels at hour twenty. A timer that produces meaningful choice on paper can read as an anxiety tax
in practice, and the players who feel that have now got a one-click answer.

Console players do not. On PS5, Xbox Series and Switch 2 the clock is the clock, and the only
thing that changes it is a patch. Rebel Wolves has not indicated it plans one.
