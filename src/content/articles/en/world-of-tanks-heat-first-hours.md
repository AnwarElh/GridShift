HEAT is going to feel familiar for about four minutes and then stop. It is a Wargaming game about
tanks, and almost everything structural around the tanks has changed. The players having the worst
time with it are, predictably, the ones with the most World of Tanks hours.

## Unlearn the vehicle-first instinct

In World of Tanks the vehicle is the decision. You research a line, you climb it, and the tank you
bring determines what you do.

In HEAT the Agent is the decision. The Agent belongs to a role — Defender, Assault or Marksman — and
the role determines which tanks are available to you and what your team expects. The tank is chosen
inside a decision you have already made.

Practically: stop asking "which tank should I take" and start asking "what does my team need from
this slot". The answer to the second question makes the first one nearly automatic.

## The habits that still work

Not everything transfers badly. Keep these:

- **Reading the map for firing lanes.** Still the highest-value skill in the game, and it is
  identical.
- **Angling and using terrain.** The armour interaction is not World of Tanks' model, but the
  principle that a hull pointed correctly survives longer is intact.
- **Patience on the first push.** Committing early into an unscouted lane loses matches here as
  reliably as it ever did.
- **Watching the minimap for absences.** Where the enemy is *not* is still the most useful thing on
  your screen.

## The habits that will get you killed

- **Treating range as safety.** Marksman is a role with a job — range, information, patience — and
  not a way to avoid the fight. A Marksman playing at brawling distance has the passives and skill of
  a specialist and the survivability of neither. This is the single most common new-player error.
- **Grinding towards something.** There is nothing to grind towards. Fifteen tanks and eight Agents,
  available without a research ladder. If you are playing badly now in order to play well later,
  there is no later. Play the role properly from match one.
- **Playing every role like a Defender.** Twenty years of tank games teach survival first. Assault's
  job is to close, and an Assault player who plays cautiously is a slow Defender with less armour.
- **Ignoring your Agent's skill.** Each Agent brings a unique skill and a passive on top of the tank.
  Players from World of Tanks routinely forget these exist for entire matches, because their previous
  game had no equivalent.

## Play all three roles before you settle

Spend the first hours deliberately: a few matches as each of Chopper, Kent and Hound — the three
introductory Agents, one per role — before you decide what you are. It costs nothing, there is no
progression penalty for switching, and the alternative is committing to a role because it looked
comfortable in a menu.

The useful question after that sample is not "which was most fun" but "which one's failures did I
understand". A role you lose in and can explain why is a role you will improve at.

## What to do with hour ten

By then you will know your role, and the next improvement is almost entirely about your team. Watch
where the Defenders on your team set up and play off them. If nobody on your side is holding space,
that is what is missing, and a competent Defender fixes more losses than a good Marksman does.

The progression system is not going to carry you here. There is not one. What is left is the part
World of Tanks always had underneath the tech tree, with the tech tree removed.
