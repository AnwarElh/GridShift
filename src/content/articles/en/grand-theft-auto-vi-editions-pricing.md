Grand Theft Auto VI is dated November 19, 2026, and the pre-order page is open. Before you use it,
it is worth separating what Rockstar has announced from what the page's structure is encouraging you
to assume.

## What has actually been announced

Three things, and only three.

- An **Ultimate Edition at $99.99**, containing premium vehicles, weapons and apparel threaded
  through the story.
- A **Vintage Vice City Pack**, included with any pre-order or purchase made **before November 20,
  2026**.
- **A free month of GTA+** for digital pre-orders.

That is the announced list. Everything else — the standard edition's price, what a physical release
looks like, whether there will be more tiers — has not been confirmed.

## What $99.99 buys

The Ultimate Edition's contents are described as premium vehicles, weapons and apparel that appear
through the story rather than being handed over in a menu at the start.

That framing is worth pausing on, because it cuts both ways. In its favour: content integrated into
the campaign is better design than a bundle of items dumped into your inventory before you have a
reason to want them. Against it: content integrated into the campaign is also content you cannot
evaluate, ignore, or return, and it makes the thing you are paying an extra premium for harder to
separate from the game you were buying anyway.

There is no published breakdown of how many vehicles, how many weapons, or how much apparel. There is
no announced story content, no announced expansion, and no announced season pass attached to the
tier. On the information available today, $99.99 buys the game plus a cosmetic and equipment layer of
undisclosed size.

## The bonuses are a deadline, not a discount

The Vintage Vice City Pack is included with **any** pre-order or purchase before November 20, 2026.
Read that date again: it is the day *after* release.

So the pack is not a pre-order exclusive in the usual sense. You can buy the game on launch day, or
the morning after, and still get it. What the November 20 cutoff actually does is remove the reason
to wait — not the reason to wait until launch, but the reason to wait until reviews, until
performance reports, until you know whether the version you want runs the way you want.

That is the mechanism worth naming. A deadline set one day past release is a deadline designed to
land before anybody can tell you what they think of the game.

The free month of GTA+ for digital pre-orders works the same way. It is a real thing with a real
value, and it is attached specifically to buying before you know anything.

## The standard edition has no price, and that matters

Rockstar has confirmed $99.99 for the Ultimate Edition and has not confirmed a price for the
standard one.

Announcing the top tier first is a normal sequence and it is also an anchoring one: $99.99 becomes
the number in your head before the base price exists to be compared with it. Whatever the standard
edition turns out to cost, it will be read as "cheaper than $99.99" rather than on its own terms.

The practical consequence is simple. You currently cannot calculate the premium you are being asked
to pay, because one of the two numbers has not been published. Anyone telling you the Ultimate
Edition is or is not good value is guessing at the arithmetic.

## The things that are not in any edition

Worth listing plainly, because the pre-order page does not say them:

- **There is no PC version announced.** Not in any edition, at any price. The platforms are PS5 and
  Xbox Series X|S.
- **No edition includes a review.** The first independent verdicts will arrive around launch, after
  the Vintage Vice City Pack window has closed for anybody buying at midnight.
- **No performance details have been published.** Frame rate modes, resolution targets, install size
  — none of it is announced, and all of it is the sort of thing that decides which console version
  someone should buy.

## What we would do

Pre-ordering a Rockstar game is a low-risk bet in the sense that the game will exist and will be
finished; this is not a studio with a history of shipping nothing. The risk is narrower: you are
paying a $99.99-tier premium for a content layer whose size has not been disclosed, in order to
secure a bonus pack that is available to anyone who buys before November 20 anyway.

If you already know you want the game on day one, buying the standard edition when it is priced — or
on launch day itself — gets you the same Vintage Vice City Pack and keeps the extra money in your
pocket until someone has told you what the game is like.

If you want the Ultimate Edition's contents, buy it. That is a legitimate preference and there is
nothing wrong with wanting more things in a game you are going to play for a hundred hours. Just buy
it because you want the contents, not because of a date that expires the day after everyone else
finds out.
