Neverness to Everness looks like an open-world city game with combat attached, and for a good number
of hours you can play it that way. You will hit a wall. The combat has one mechanic underneath it
that everything else exists to serve, and it is not obvious from the tutorial.

## The bar is the point

During a fight a small bar fills. When it is full, swapping to another Esper via an entry attack
triggers an **Esper Cycle** — a reaction that does considerably more damage than simply continuing
to attack with the character you already have out. Players who treat swapping as a way to rescue a
low-health character are leaving most of their damage unused.

## Which reaction you get depends on the pair

The effect that fires is determined by the ability combination of the two Espers being swapped
between. This is why roster advice in NTE is always about pairs rather than individual units: a
strong Esper next to the wrong partner produces a weaker cycle than two moderate ones that match.

## Build the squad around roles, then pairs

Your squad is four Espers, each in one of three roles — Damage, Buff or Survival. A team needs at
least one of each; quest content puts you against several enemies at once and a squad of four damage
dealers dies to the first fight that does not end quickly. Sort the roles first, then arrange the
order so your cycles land between compatible pairs.

## The city is not filler

Vehicles, racing, mahjong, property and running a business sit under the City Tycoon side of the
game. It is genuinely a second mode rather than decoration — but it is not what gets you through the
combat content, and new players regularly spend their first evening on it and wonder why their team
is behind.
