Welove Games' Three Kingdoms borrows the MOBA's skeleton — a tactical map with left, middle and
right lanes — and then does something a MOBA does not: it scales the fight to 100 versus 100 and
takes the single champion away from you. You are commanding, not piloting.

## Squads, not a character

You train three kinds of unit: civilian, warrior and hero squads. That composition is your real
loadout. A player thinking in MOBA terms looks for their champion's power spike; a player thinking
correctly here looks at whether their squad mix can hold a lane against what is coming down it.

## Lanes still decide the shape of the game

Left, middle and right behave as you would expect — they are where pressure is applied and where it
has to be answered. What differs is scale. At a hundred players a side, a lane is not won by one
person outplaying another; it is won by which side committed the right squads to it and kept them
supplied.

## Cities are the win condition and the economy

Capturing a city does not just move a marker. Captured cities send out helper forces of their own,
which means every capture compounds: it adds pressure to the lane it sits on without costing you
squads you have already committed elsewhere. Trading a fight to take a city is usually correct.
Trading a city to win a fight usually is not.

## Where new players go wrong

Treating a hundred-a-side battle as a big deathmatch. The scale is the point: the map rewards
coordinated lane commitment and city capture, and punishes the whole server chasing one fight in the
middle.
