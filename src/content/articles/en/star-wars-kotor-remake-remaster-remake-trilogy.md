Three games this site covers arrive with three different words attached, and this autumn happens to
put all three on sale at once. They are not synonyms. The difference decides how much of the original
survives, how much you pay, and how long you wait.

## Remaster: the same game, running better

A remaster takes the **existing game** and improves how it is presented. The assets, the code and the
design are the original's; what changes is resolution, lighting, textures, frame rate and, sometimes,
quality-of-life systems.

**The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt — Remastered** (29 September) is the textbook case, and an unusually
generous one. CD PROJEKT RED's own list covers enhanced path tracing, updated dynamic lighting,
higher-resolution textures, improved foliage, refined targeting, new gap-closing animations, revamped
mount handling, a skill tree revamp and transmogrification.

That last group is worth flagging, because a remaster that touches **combat targeting and the skill
tree** is doing more than a remaster usually does. The line between a generous remaster and a light
remake is genuinely blurry, and this sits close to it.

What tells you it is still a remaster: **it is free for existing owners**, and it runs on the same
game. You are not being asked to buy the story again.

## Remake: rebuilt, and free to change what it means

A remake **rebuilds the game** — new engine, new assets, frequently new systems. It is not obliged to
preserve the original's design, and the interesting ones do not.

**Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic – Remake** is the strict case. Announced in September 2021,
halted in July 2022, handed to Saber Interactive and rebuilt from scratch across three years. It is a
remake by definition and, at the time of writing, by nothing else: **no gameplay has ever been shown
and there is no release window**.

The distinction matters most here. *KOTOR*'s 2003 combat is a turn-based d20 system presented in real
time — the part of the original that has aged worst. A remaster would be obliged to keep it. A
remake is free to replace it entirely. That freedom is the reason the project is interesting and
also the reason it is hard: replacing it means re-deciding what the game is.

## Remake trilogy: a fourth category the industry invented

**Final Fantasy VII Remake** is called a remake and is not one, in the sense above.

The trilogy — *Remake* (2020), *Rebirth* (2024), *Revelation* (8 April 2027) — reimagines the 1997
original across three full-length games. It changes the story rather than restaging it. A 1997 game
of roughly forty hours becomes three games of eighty to a hundred each.

That is not a remaster and not really a remake either. It is closer to an adaptation: the source is
recognisable, the events are rearranged, and the new work makes claims the original did not.

The practical consequence is the one buyers feel: **you cannot start in the middle and you cannot
finish early**. Three purchases, seven years of waiting, and a conclusion that only arrives in 2027.

## How to tell which one you are being sold

Four questions that settle it faster than the marketing does:

1. **Is it free for existing owners?** If yes, it is a remaster. Nobody gives away a rebuild.
2. **Does it run on the original engine?** Remaster. New engine means remake.
3. **Can the story change?** A remaster cannot. A remake may. A "remake trilogy" already has.
4. **How many games is it?** More than one means the scope changed, and the word is doing work
   marketing chose for it.

## Why the label changes the price and the wait

| | The Witcher 3 — Remastered | KOTOR – Remake | FF7 Remake Trilogy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type | Remaster | Remake | Reimagining across 3 games |
| Cost to existing owners | **Free** | Unknown | Three full purchases |
| Date | 29 Sept 2026 | **None announced** | 2020 · 2024 · 8 Apr 2027 |
| Original's design | Preserved, tuned | Free to replace | Substantially rewritten |
| Announced | Aug 2026 | Sept 2021 | 2015 |

The pattern is consistent. **A remaster ships fast and often free, because the expensive work is
already done.** *The Witcher 3*'s was announced in August and ships in September.

**A remake takes years and may not arrive.** *KOTOR*'s was announced five years ago and has shown
nothing.

**A reimagining takes a decade and three purchases.** *Final Fantasy VII*'s was confirmed as
multi-part in 2015 and concludes in 2027.

None of the three is better than the others as a category. But knowing which one you are looking at
tells you, immediately, whether to expect it this month, this decade, or at all.
