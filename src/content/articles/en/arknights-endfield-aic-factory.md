Endfield is three games bolted together: real-time squad combat with four operators, open sandbox
regions to cross, and an Automated Industry Complex that runs whether or not you are looking at it.
New players treat the third one as a side activity. It is not. Combat power, exploration range and
operator progression all draw on what the AIC produces.

## Build a compact loop first

The instinct is to sprawl. Resist it. A tight loop around the PAC — mining into smelting into
crafting — is worth more early than a wide layout you cannot power. Long belt runs cost you
materials you do not have yet and make problems harder to see.

## Get power stable before you get clever

Every production chain stops when power dips. Establish reliable power and basic mining automation
before adding a third or fourth product line. A small complex that runs continuously out-produces a
large one that stalls whenever you queue something expensive.

## Spend AIC Plan upgrades in order

The four branches — Resourcing, Logistics, Processing and Power — do not deserve equal attention at
the start. Tier I and Tier II across the board beats rushing one branch deep. You are buying
throughput, and throughput is limited by whichever stage is slowest.

## What the factory is actually for

Mining rigs pull raw ore, smelters turn it into bars, factories turn bars into gear parts,
construction materials and consumables. That last category is the one that reaches your squad. If
your operators feel underpowered, the answer is usually upstream, in a production line that is not
keeping up — not in the gacha.
