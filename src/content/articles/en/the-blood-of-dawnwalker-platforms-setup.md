The Blood of Dawnwalker released yesterday on PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S and PC through Steam,
the Xbox app and GOG. Four things about the setup are worth getting right before you start a
thirty-day run you will not want to restart.

## Install the day-one patch before you start

Rebel Wolves originally announced the console versions at a **40 FPS cap**. After sustained
criticism, the studio changed position on **August 24** — "we heard your feedback" — and committed to
a **60 FPS mode delivered in a day-one patch**.

So the performance mode is not in the version on the disc or in the base download. Check that the
patch has actually applied before your first session, and check the display settings menu rather than
assuming: a mode added late in the cycle is a mode that may not be the default.

Which you should use is a straightforward question here. Dawnwalker's combat is four-directional
attacking and blocking, which is a system that reads through animation timing rather than through
twitch reaction. It is playable and pleasant at 40. It is clearly better at 60, and after two days
with both I would take the frame rate over whatever the fidelity mode is protecting. If you started
on 40 and have got used to it, switching mid-run is not disruptive — nothing about the game's systems
depends on it.

## Xbox Series S is the exception

The 60fps commitment **excludes Xbox Series S**. Series S players are on the 40fps cap and, as of
launch, there is no announced plan to change that.

That is not a reason to skip the game on Series S — 40 is a deliberate target, not a broken one — but
it does mean the Series S version is the one release in the line-up whose performance story is
finished rather than still moving.

## Difficulty is a time decision, not just a combat one

There are **four difficulty levels**. In most RPGs choosing one is purely a question of how much
resistance you want. Here it interacts with the game's central system, and nobody tells you.

Levelling up **costs time segments**. You have thirty days of eight segments each, and every level
you buy is a quest you did not do. So a higher difficulty does not only mean harder fights: it means
more pressure to spend segments on power, which means fewer segments left for the story. A lower
difficulty is, quite literally, more content.

The practical advice:

- **If this is your first run**, take a middle setting. The structure is the point of this game and
  you want your segments going into quests, not into staying alive.
- **If you want the harder version of the fights**, accept in advance that you will see less of the
  world, and pick your quests earlier and more decisively.
- **Do not pick a difficulty planning to change it later.** You can, but the segments you already
  spent on levels do not come back.

## Editions: what the money buys

Three, and they are unusually easy to separate:

- **Standard.** The game.
- **Eclipse Edition.** Adds the soundtrack and a digital comic. The soundtrack is by Nikola
  Kołodziejczyk, Piotr Musiał and Mikolai Stroinski, which is a genuinely strong credit, and it is
  the only version of this upgrade that is about the game rather than about a shelf.
- **Collector's Edition.** Physical versions of the above plus a world map and a figure.

Nothing in any edition changes the game itself — there is no content, no early access and no
gameplay advantage attached. Buy the tier that matches how much you want a physical object.

## Two PC builds, one game

There are **two PC builds carrying different age ratings by region**. Which one you get is determined
by where you buy, not by a setting, and the storefronts are Steam, the Xbox app and GOG.

If you care about which version you are getting, this is worth checking on the store page before
purchase rather than discovering afterwards. We have not compared the two builds and are not going to
tell you they are identical.

## Shrines are the setting nobody tells you about

Not a menu option, but it belongs in a setup piece because it changes how you play from hour one:
**wayside shrines are fast travel, levelling and item storage in one place.**

That bundling matters. Because levelling costs segments and shrines are where levelling happens, your
route around the map and your progression schedule are the same decision. Get in the habit of
noticing shrines while you explore — exploration is free, remember — so that when you do decide to
buy a level, the place that sells it is already on your map and already unlocked as a travel point.
