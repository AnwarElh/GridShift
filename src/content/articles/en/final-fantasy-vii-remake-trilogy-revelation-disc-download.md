*Final Fantasy VII Revelation* is getting a physical release on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, but the
disc will not hold the whole game: part of it downloads during installation. Director **Naoki
Hamaguchi** has now explained why, in an interview with **IGN** published this week, and the
explanation is already drawing pushback.

According to Hamaguchi, the team first discussed "using the same disc size as we did for, say,
*FFVII Rebirth*", and concluded that doing so "would mean that we would have to make a compromise in
terms of the fidelity and the quality of the game." What it settled on instead, in his words, was a
disc "with certain sections of the game being a digital download format." He said he understood
not every player would be happy, and that he was glad to be shipping a disc at all while the market
moves the other way.

## The disc was never going to be complete

The download itself is not news. **Push Square** reported on September 3 that the PS5 version ships
on a single disc with a required download, and we flagged it in our
[edition breakdown](/setup/final-fantasy-vii-remake-trilogy-revelation-editions/) the next day. In
July, as Kotaku points out, Hamaguchi had only promised to work towards "some kind of physical
edition" — a phrase that reads rather more carefully chosen now. What is new is the reason.

## Where the explanation gets thin

The comparison Hamaguchi picked is the problem. *Rebirth* did not fit on one disc either: it shipped
on two, one to install from and one to play, for an install of nearly 150GB. A PS5 disc holds up to
100GB. Nothing in the interview explains why *Revelation* could not do what *Rebirth* did and add a
disc.

Kotaku reports that Hamaguchi's post on the subject on X has picked up a Community Note making
exactly that point: multi-disc PS5 releases are supported, have been used for large games, and so
avoiding them is not a technical necessity.

It is worth keeping the three layers apart. Documented: the disc needs a download, and *Rebirth*
came on two. Claimed by Hamaguchi: matching *Rebirth*'s format would have cost the game quality.
Inference: read generously, "the same disc size" means two discs, and *Revelation* is larger than
200GB — which would be news in itself, and which Square Enix has not said. Even then, the fix is a
third disc. That is a cost decision, not a fidelity one, and nobody outside the company can tell you
how it was weighed.

## Why the timing stings

*Revelation* arrives on **April 8, 2027**, in what IGN calls the last year Sony produces physical
PlayStation discs; Kotaku has new disc production ending in 2028. A disc that cannot install the
full game without Sony's or Microsoft's servers is, as Kotaku argues, closer to the game-key card
*Revelation* ships as on Switch 2 than to the discs the first two games came on.

Square Enix has given no sign it will change course, and pre-orders opened straight after the
State of Play. On a shelf, the box will look like the other two. It just won't do the same job.
