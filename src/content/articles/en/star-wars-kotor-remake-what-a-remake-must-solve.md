A note before anything else: this article contains no information about the remake, because there is
none. Saber Interactive has said it is still in development and nothing further. What follows is an
argument about the 2003 game and the problems it hands to anyone who tries to rebuild it — framed as
questions, because that is all they can honestly be.

It also discusses the structure of KOTOR's ending in general terms. Nothing is named, but if you
have never played it and intend to, stop at the third heading.

## Fidelity is the least interesting question

Every remake discussion collapses into the same argument about how faithful it should look, and it
is the argument that matters least. New art is the part of this job with the most precedent, the
clearest process, and the lowest risk of getting it structurally wrong. A studio that rebuilds
Taris and the Endar Spire to a 2026 standard has done difficult work, not *uncertain* work.

The uncertain work is underneath. KOTOR is a 2003 design, and three of its load-bearing decisions do
not survive being modernised without someone making a real choice about them.

## The combat is d20 wearing a real-time coat

KOTOR's combat presents as real-time action and is not. It is the **d20** tabletop ruleset resolved
in rounds — attack rolls against defence, damage dice, modifiers from your build — with animations
draped over the numbers and a pause button that exposes the queue underneath. Your character sheet,
not your hands, decides most encounters.

That is a coherent design. It is also almost exactly the opposite of what a 2026 audience assumes
when a Star Wars game shows them a lightsaber.

The problem is that the two obvious paths both cost something real:

- **Keep the dice.** The build matters, the RPG remains an RPG, and the combat stays a systems
  puzzle. But a remake that looks contemporary and plays as a round-based dice game creates a
  mismatch between what the presentation promises and what the input does — the exact mismatch that
  confuses new players in the original today.
- **Make it action combat.** It reads correctly, it feels like the marketing, and it detonates the
  character-building layer. If a skilled player can win a fight the numbers say they should lose,
  every stat allocation in the game means less than it did.

There is a third path — a modernised tactical layer that is honest about being tactical — and it is
harder than either. Which of the three Saber has taken is unknown. It is the single most
consequential thing we do not know about this project, and no statement from the studio has come
anywhere near it.

## The second half runs out of road

The standard reading of KOTOR, held for two decades, is that its back half is compressed. The
opening act is a long, careful tutorial; the middle is the game at its best; and then the ending
arrives faster and thinner than what preceded it, with the planet-by-planet structure giving way to
a run at the finish.

This is where a remake has its clearest opportunity and its clearest trap.

The opportunity is obvious: the material exists to fill that space, and a modern budget could
actually build it. The trap is that expanding the weakest stretch of a beloved game means writing
new content into a story people already have opinions about — and every hour added is an hour that
did not exist in the thing they are asking you to be faithful to. A remake that pads the back half
with modern open-world filler would be worse than the compressed original, not better.

The honest question is not *will they expand it* but *do they have the writing to expand it with*.
Nobody outside the project can answer that, and nothing has been said that gestures at it.

## The twist is an information system

KOTOR's famous late-game reveal is not a cutscene. It is the payoff of an information-control system
that runs across the whole game — what the player is told, what characters know and do not say, what
the camera never quite shows you, and what a 2003 presentation could simply decline to depict.

Restraint was cheap in 2003. Low-polygon faces do not leak. Fixed camera framing does not
accidentally reveal. Dialogue delivered in a limited number of takes does not carry the micro-
expression that gives a lie away.

A cinematic 2026 presentation inverts every one of those. Performance capture, facial animation,
dynamic cameras and full voice direction are all technologies for *conveying more*, and this
particular story depends on conveying less. Every upgrade in fidelity is a new place for the secret
to escape — a held glance, a line reading with the wrong weight, a camera that lingers where the
original had nothing to linger on.

That is a genuinely hard direction problem, and it is not solved by being careful. It has to be
designed for: deciding, scene by scene, what the new presentation is permitted to know. Whether
Saber has framed it that way is exactly the sort of thing a re-reveal trailer will not tell you.

## What "rebuilt from scratch" can and cannot mean

Aspyr's version was halted in July 2022. Saber took the project over and, by every account given
since, restarted it. Roughly three years have passed.

"From scratch" is a phrase that covers a wide range. It can mean new engine, new code, new art, and
the 2003 script and structure as the design document. It can also mean the systems themselves were
reopened. The first is a remake in the ordinary sense; the second is a different game wearing the
name, and both have shipped under the word "remake" in the last decade.

What three years of rebuild tells us is only about scale. It says the project is large enough to
have justified starting over rather than salvaging, and that whatever exists now was made by a team
that chose its own foundations. It does not tell us what those foundations are, or which of the
three problems above they were chosen to solve.

## The questions to ask when it is finally shown

When something is eventually shown — whenever that is — the trailer will answer the easy question
and dodge the hard ones. So the checklist is short:

- **Does a hit connect because of a roll or because of an input?** Watch for misses.
- **Is there a character sheet, and does the footage suggest it decides anything?**
- **Is the structure still planet-by-planet, and how much of the runtime sits after the midpoint?**
- **How much does the camera show of faces in conversation?** A remake that has thought about the
  twist will be visibly disciplined about it, and one that has not will look like every other
  cinematic RPG.

Until then, everything else is decoration on an announcement from 2021.
