It has to be said up front: Echo Divide starts badly. Not badly made — badly calibrated. The game
puts its most punishing systems in front of its best ideas, and probably loses half its audience
before the second traversal ability opens.

Those who get past that point find something else. The world folds back on itself, the shortcuts
reveal themselves, and the progression loop becomes one of the clearest in the genre.

## Six hours to get through

The problem is not the difficulty, it is the lack of tools. With no movement ability, every mistake
costs three minutes of walking. The first act's economy, before patch 4.2a, left no margin at all:
one bad fight could cost an hour.

<Ad />

## Then a game of the highest order

Once traversal is yours, Echo Divide unfolds what it was holding back: a dense world, shortcuts
that reward attention, and writing that never repeats the same information twice. Co-op does not
lower the difficulty, it distributes it — that is rare, and very well done.

## On consoles

Performance remains unstable on base consoles: regular drops below 30 frames per second in district
three. Patch 4.2a changed nothing there. On PC and on the pro models, nothing to report.
