Most survival games treat a base as storage with defences bolted on. Once Human treats it as a place
of work, and the workers are the anomalous creatures you capture out in the world. Getting this right
early is the largest single accelerator available to a new character.

## What a Deviation actually is

Deviations are captured anomalies, stored in containment units and placed in your territory, where
each performs a function continuously. Some produce resources. Some process or refine. Some cook.
Some defend the base against raids. Some can be taken into the field with you.

The important distinction from a crafting station is that a Deviation *keeps working*. A station is a
thing you visit; a Deviation is a thing that runs. That changes what a base is for — it is not
somewhere you return to in order to craft, it is somewhere that has been producing while you were
away.

## The three jobs that matter early

In a fresh scenario, the value order is not the same as the rarity order. Three roles pay for
themselves fastest:

1. **Production.** A Deviation that generates a base resource on a timer converts your offline hours
   into materials. Everything else in your build waits on materials. This is the first slot to fill.
2. **Food and water processing.** Both are consumed constantly, and both are the most common reason a
   new player interrupts what they were doing to go and solve a chore. Automate the chore.
3. **Defence.** Lower priority than the community usually claims in early PvE, and non-negotiable
   before you leave a well-stocked base unattended in any scenario with raids.

Combat Deviations you take into the field are excellent and are not the first thing to invest in.
Field power is seasonal; a base that produces while you are logged out compounds.

## Happiness is a real mechanic

Deviations have satisfaction levels, and an unhappy one is a less productive one. Their needs are
driven by their placement and the state of your territory — space, appropriate surroundings, the
facilities they expect.

The practical version: do not cram every containment unit into one room because it is tidy. Check the
satisfaction indicator after placing anything, and treat a drop as a problem to solve rather than a
cosmetic complaint. A base of unhappy Deviations produces noticeably less than the same base arranged
with a little more room, and most players never look at the number.

## Build the base around the workforce

The layout mistake nearly everyone makes on their first territory is to design the building, then
find places to put the Deviations. Invert it.

Decide which Deviations are running, put them where they will be satisfied and where their outputs
are near the things that consume those outputs, and build the walls around that. A base is a
production line before it is a house. The aesthetics survive that constraint perfectly well — and
since your territory is seasonal anyway, function is the part worth optimising.

## What to prioritise in a fresh scenario

Blueprints carry across seasons; Deviations do not. Every scenario you re-catch, which means the
early-game capture route is something worth having a plan for rather than improvising.

Have a production Deviation in place before you invest in weapon upgrades. Get food and water off
your to-do list next. Then defence, sized to the ruleset you chose. Only after that is the field
roster worth spending on — by which point you will have the materials to do it properly, because
something has been working while you were out.
