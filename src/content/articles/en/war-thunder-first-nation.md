Every new War Thunder player asks which nation has the best tanks, gets ten contradictory answers,
picks one, and spreads their research across four trees within a fortnight. The question is wrong,
and so is what it leads to.

## Why the vehicle is the wrong unit of decision

You do not bring a tank to a Realistic Battle. You bring a lineup — several vehicles at similar
Battle Ratings that you can respawn into as you lose them. A match where you die once and leave is a
match where you earned a fraction of the Research Points and paid a repair bill anyway.

So the useful question is not "which nation has the best heavy tank at 6.7". It is "at which Battle
Rating can this nation field four or five vehicles I want to play, including something that can
answer aircraft?" A nation with one exceptional tank and nothing beside it is worse for a new account
than a nation with five adequate ones.

## Pick a domain before you pick a nation

Ground, air and naval share a client and a currency but not a skill set. Committing to one for your
first fifty hours is the highest-value decision you will make, because the research costs are
per-vehicle and spreading them out means arriving nowhere in three trees at once.

Ground Realistic is the most common entry point and the most forgiving of slow reactions. Air
Realistic is the steepest — you are fighting the flight model and the enemy simultaneously. Naval is
the smallest population and the least documented; it is a fine place to be, but not first.

## The nations that are kind to a new account

**Germany** and **the USSR** are the standard recommendations for ground, and they earn it for an
unglamorous reason: both have dense, well-populated trees with complete lineups at almost every
Battle Rating, so you are never researching a gap. Their low and mid brackets are the most
extensively documented in the community, which matters when you are trying to work out why you
bounced.

**Sweden** is the quiet answer. The tree is smaller, which sounds like a drawback and is not — fewer
dead-end branches, and several brackets where the lineup is unusually strong for its rating.

**The USA** is a good air tree and a demanding ground one. If you know you want aircraft, start here.

## The ones to avoid first, and why

**Japan**, **China**, **Italy** and **France** are not bad trees. They are trees with holes: Battle
Ratings where the nation cannot field a full lineup, or where the available vehicles are specialised
enough that they punish inexperience. Israel is the same case in a smaller tree. Every one of them is
worth playing eventually and none of them is worth learning the game in.

**Britain** deserves its own note. The guns and the armour are genuinely good, the ammunition
historically was not, and the tree asks you to understand what your shell actually does before you
fire it. It is a rewarding second nation and a punishing first.

## One nation, one domain, one bracket

The single most effective piece of advice in this game: research one nation, in one domain, until you
have a lineup you enjoy at one Battle Rating — then stop and play it for a while.

Players who progress fastest are not the ones who unlock the most vehicles. They are the ones who
spend fifty matches at the same rating, learning which shells work on which plates, where the flanks
are on eight maps, and what their own vehicles cannot do. That knowledge transfers to every other
tree. An unlocked tank you do not understand does not.
