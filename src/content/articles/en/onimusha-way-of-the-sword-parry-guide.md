Onimusha: Way of the Sword is not a Soulslike and does not want to be one, which means most of the
reflexes you brought with you are wrong. It is a parry game where the parry does two things, and a
stamina game where the stamina bar you care about belongs to the other guy. Here is the whole system
in the order it is worth learning it.

## Guard is a floor, not a strategy

The game gives you a **Guard** stance that blocks from all directions. It works. It will get you
through the opening hours and it is there deliberately — this is the accessibility floor Capcom built
in so that the combat is legible to someone who is not going to spend forty hours on it.

It is also a trap if you stay there. Guarding costs you nothing and gains you nothing: no stamina
damage to the enemy, no buffs, no route to an execution. Every fight you win from Guard is a fight
you won slowly. Treat it as the thing you fall back to when a crowd gets away from you, not as your
default posture.

## The parry has a second half

Parry an incoming attack and you get the usual: the hit is stopped, the attacker is briefly open. The
part the game does not sell properly is that you can **hold the parry longer** and *steer* — the
enemy stumbles in a direction you choose rather than a direction the animation chose.

That converts a defensive input into a positioning tool, and it is the single largest jump in your
play. Concretely:

- **Against one enemy near a wall or a step**, steer them into it. The recovery is longer and you get
  a free approach.
- **Against three enemies**, steer the one you parried into the path of the other two. You are not
  killing him, you are spending him. Two seconds of an inconvenienced group is worth more than one
  extra hit.
- **In a doorway or a narrow room**, steer *towards* the choke rather than away from it. Fights in
  corridors are winnable in a way fights in open rooms are not, and you can manufacture a corridor.

Projectiles can be deflected with the same input. Archers stop being a reason to break off an
engagement.

## Stamina is a target, not a bar

Both striking and parrying drain an enemy's stamina. Empty it and you get a **Break Issen** — an
instant, dismembering execution.

This is the sentence that reorganises how you fight: you are not trying to reduce an enemy's health,
you are trying to empty a resource you can see, and parrying does it as well as attacking. A defensive
player and an aggressive player arrive at the same execution by different routes, which is why the
combat holds up across skill levels.

The practical consequence for a new player: **stop disengaging after a successful parry.** The
instinct from other games is to parry, hit once, and back off. Here, backing off refunds the enemy's
stamina progress in spirit if not in fact — you have given up the pressure that was about to pay out.
Parry, and stay.

## Chain, and the game pays you

Consecutive clean parries and dodges grant buffs: chained executions, multi-hit attacks. The exact
rewards escalate with the run of successes, so a fight where you never eat a hit is qualitatively
different from a fight where you eat two.

That is worth knowing because it changes what a mistake costs. A single hit taken is a small health
loss and a large tempo loss. In practice, if you have a good chain running and you are unsure whether
you can parry the next attack, Guard is the correct answer — it keeps you alive but it does not
preserve the chain, so use it knowing you have spent something.

## The soul colours, in one pass

Your gauntlet is sentient and it eats souls. There are three:

- **Yellow — health.** Absorb when hurt. That is all there is to it.
- **Red — upgrade currency.** Permanent progression. Nothing tactical about it in the moment.
- **Blue — Oni Armaments.** These are the high-damage special weapons, and here is the part worth
  memorising: **hitting with an Oni Armament returns yellow souls.** Blue is therefore not a separate
  meter, it is a way of converting a burst of damage into healing.

Which means blue is worth saving for the moments when you are both under pressure and short on
health, not for the moments when you want a boss to die faster. Those are different moments and
players confuse them constantly in the first ten hours.

**Oni Vision** reveals enemy positions. Use it before entering a space, not during a fight — its
value is planning, and the game rarely hides anything from you mid-combat that you needed to see.

## The choice a boss makes you make

Break Issen on a boss does **not** kill it. Instead the game hands you a decision: take **massive
damage**, or take **more souls**.

There is no universally right answer, and that is the point. The reasonable default:

- **Take the damage** if you are near the end of the fight, low on health, or the boss has a phase
  you would rather skip. Massive damage is about *this* fight.
- **Take the souls** if you are comfortable, early in the encounter, or you know a hard stretch is
  coming. Souls are about the *next three* fights.

The mistake to avoid is treating it as a reward screen and always pressing the same button. It is a
loan decision — you are choosing between spending now and being richer later — and the boss you are
currently fighting is rarely the one that will kill you.
