The habit twenty years of Wargaming games builds is to pick a vehicle. HEAT inverts it. You choose an
Agent, that Agent belongs to one of three roles, and the role determines which tanks you can take and
what the team expects from you. Each Agent brings a unique skill and a passive trait on top.

## The three roles, and what they actually ask

**Defender** holds space. High survivability, firepower to punish anyone who tests it, and reduced
mobility as the cost. The job is absorbing attention so the rest of the team can move.

**Assault** is speed and flanks. High-velocity, at its best in the middle of a fight, finishing
openings before the enemy can reset the line.

**Marksman** is range, information and patience. You hunt from distance and from height, drawing
enemies out without being spotted yourself.

## Start with the Agent that matches how you already play

Chopper, Kent and Hound are the three introductory Agents, one per role. Chopper is the Defender —
high health, slow. Kent is straightforward Assault aggression. Hound is the Marksman, and rewards
patience rather than reflexes.

There is no wrong first pick, but there is a wasteful one: taking Hound because sniping looks safe,
then playing him at brawling range. The passive and the skill are built around the role. Fight the
role and you get the worst of both.

## What is not here

No tech tree grind, no vehicle progression ladder to climb before the game opens up. Fifteen tanks
and eight Agents at launch, with full cross-platform play and progression across PC, Steam Deck, PS5,
Xbox Series and GeForce NOW. Whether that is liberating or thin depends on what you wanted from it.
