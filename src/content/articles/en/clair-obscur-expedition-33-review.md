Sixteen months after release, Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 has won more or less everything a video
game can win. Nine awards at The Game Awards 2025 — the most in the show's history. Game of the Year
at DICE, at the GDC Awards, and at BAFTA. Eight million copies as of April 2026. A debut game, from a
studio nobody had heard of, published by Kepler Interactive.

That is exactly why this review is late.

## Why a review now, and what it can honestly tell you

Scoring this game in December 2025 would have been scoring a wave. Everyone was writing the same
piece at the same time, and a number published in that week measures the week. So Gridshift waited
until the release existed in a settled state — after the free Thank You Update, after the June 30,
2026 patch that took language support to twenty — and then went back to it.

What a re-check is good at is telling you whether the thing survived its own reputation, and what
changed underneath it. What it is not is a fresh set of eyes: nobody in 2026 comes to this game
without knowing what it won. Take the number below as a verdict on the current build, not as a
pretence that the last year did not happen.

## The acclaim is unusually shaped

Awards sweeps are common enough. The shape of this one is not.

Thirteen nominations at The Game Awards 2025 converted into nine wins, which is the record. Eight
nominations at the 29th DICE Awards converted into five, including Game of the Year. The 26th GDC
Awards — the one voted by developers — gave it Game of the Year plus Best Debut, Best Visual Art,
Best Narrative and Best Audio at the March 12, 2026 ceremony, having nominated it in nearly every
category on the sheet. BAFTA led with twelve nominations and tied for most wins with three,
including Best Game.

Four juries, four different constituencies — press, industry, developers, a British academy — and
no meaningful disagreement between them. That is rarer than a big night at one show. It usually
means a game did something the whole field recognised at once rather than something a particular
audience championed.

It also has a cost, and the cost is that the game has stopped being discussable. A title with nine
Game Awards on it is not a game any more; it is a settled matter. That is the single worst thing
that can happen to a piece of work sixteen months old.

## The update is the most interesting thing that happened after launch

The Thank You Update landed after the Game of the Year win, marking five million copies, and it was
free. Not a season pass, not a battle pass, not an expansion with a price on it — a free content
drop containing a new location, extra late-game bosses, new costumes and expanded language support.

Two things are worth saying about that. The first is that free post-launch content of that size is
now genuinely unusual at this price point, and it happened at the moment the studio had maximum
leverage to charge for it. The second is that the update is not a welcome mat. It is aimed squarely
at people who had already finished the game and wanted more resistance, which is not the same
audience as the eight million.

## The Endless Tower is where the game stops being polite

Inside the update sits an Endless Tower of revamped boss fights. Kotaku's headline on it was that
Clair Obscur's free DLC is "curb stomping even its best players", and that is the correct register.
This is post-campaign content built for people who have exhausted the campaign's difficulty, and it
does not ease anyone in.

If you are returning after the awards run, expecting a victory lap, this is the part that will tell
you no. That is a defensible design decision and it is also a real caveat on a recommendation: the
free update everyone praises is the part of the game most likely to end your relationship with it.

I do not think that undermines the update. I think it undermines the way the update gets described.
"Sandfall gave everyone free content" is true. "Sandfall gave everyone free content they can
finish" is not what happened.

## The Musée d'Orsay, which is not a marketing line

There is an exhibition at the Musée d'Orsay tied to this game. Write that sentence about almost any
other release of the last decade and it reads as a stunt. Here it reads as the museum noticing
something.

It is worth flagging because it is the one piece of the game's afterlife that did not come from the
industry congratulating itself. Awards juries are made of people who work in games. The Musée
d'Orsay is not, and it went and did this anyway.

## Where the re-check finds friction

Language support is the honest one. Arabic arrived on June 30, 2026, taking the total to twenty
languages — fourteen months after launch, for a game that had by then sold eight million copies and
won everything. Good that it happened. Slow that it took that long, and the delay is not explained
by studio size at that point.

The Switch 2 question is the other. There is no Switch 2 version. There is a leaker — Nate the Hate,
who called the Ocarina of Time and Star Fox remakes — reaffirming in June 2026 that it lands this
year, and there is Sandfall CEO Guillaume Broche having previously discussed the possibility. That
is not a release, a date, or a confirmation, and anybody waiting for a handheld version should be
told plainly that they are waiting on a rumour.

And there is the thing the awards did to it. A game that wins nine trophies in one night acquires a
consensus that is very difficult to argue with, and consensus is not the same as being right. It is
worth saying out loud: nothing in the prize list is evidence. It is a record of what a lot of people
concluded, quickly, together.

## The score

9.0 is a game that earned its reception and then spent its post-launch year doing the unfashionable
thing — shipping substantial content for free, adding languages, and building a difficulty tier for
the few hundred thousand people who wanted it rather than the eight million who did not.

It is not a 10, and the reason is not a flaw in the design. It is that the current build asks more
of a returning player than the acclaim suggests, and that the version most people would now want to
play — the portable one — does not exist and has never been confirmed to be coming.
