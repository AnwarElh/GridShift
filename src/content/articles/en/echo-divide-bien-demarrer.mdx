Echo Divide forces four decisions in its first twenty minutes, and three of them are permanent.
The game flags this nowhere. It presents them as flavour — a background, an affinity, a starting
district — then locks them behind a save you cannot rewind.

This guide covers the five choices that genuinely change a playthrough, in the order the game
offers them. Everything was verified on patch 4.2, then re-checked after patch 4.2a changed the
first act's economy.

## Choose your role

The character creator puts your background first, which is backwards. A background only shifts two
stat floors and a few lines of dialogue. Your **role** determines which skill tree you can reach
without a respec token — and those tokens only appear in the third district.

Solo, take Mender: it is the only role with early self-healing, and early is where solo runs die.
In co-op, split them up: the squad bonus checks that all four roles are distinct.

<Callout>
Difficulty is reversible with no penalty whatsoever. Your role is not. Save your caution for the
role screen.
</Callout>

## Set the difficulty

Echo Divide's curve is inverted. The first six hours are the hardest, for want of movement tools
and the economy to absorb one bad fight. Everything flattens out once the second traversal ability
opens.

Difficulty can be changed at any time, with no penalty and no achievement lockout — the game does
not say that either. Start lower, move up afterwards.

## Prepare the squad

The squad menu opens after the prologue, but most players do not touch it for four hours. Three
free slots are already gone by then.

Spend your first currency on stamina, not damage. Damage climbs on its own with the weapons you
find; stamina is the only real bottleneck between two checkpoints. The useful threshold has been
45 since patch 4.2a.

<Spoiler summary="Contains an act two detail">
The character you meet at the district two relay joins the squad permanently if you finished the
optional district one area. Otherwise he still shows up, but without his full tree.
</Spoiler>

## The two mistakes

The first: selling crafting components. The merchant pays well for them and the workbench only
opens in district two, which makes them look like dead weight. They are not.

The second: rushing the main path. The best early upgrade sits in an optional district one area
that closes permanently when you move to district two. With no warning at all.

## Session goals

A good first session ends with the second traversal ability unlocked, a full squad on distinct
tools, and at least one workbench upgrade. Budget three to four hours. That is the point where the
game stops fighting you.
