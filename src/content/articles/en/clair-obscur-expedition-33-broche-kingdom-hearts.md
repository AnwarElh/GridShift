**Guillaume Broche**, creative director of *Clair Obscur: Expedition 33*, appeared on **Jeux Vidéo
Club** — the show from French channel Konbini in which developers walk a game shop and talk about
what shaped them — and named *Kingdom Hearts II* one of his favourite games of all time.
**Kotaku** picked up the segment.

"I think it's in my top 5 games of all time," Broche said, per Konbini's translation. "Truly
exceptional."

## The specific thing he is praising

The quote is more useful than a favourites list, because he says what he has been looking for
since.

"There's really something about that gameplay that I've been looking for ever since *Kingdom
Hearts II*," he said. "*Kingdom Hearts III* is pretty good, but it didn't manage to recapture
everything [*II*] did in terms of gameplay, which was exceptional. There was a real depth to it.
You could spend 150 hours in the game, actually. Seriously, if you like action RPGs with cool
systems, play this game."

That is a director praising **systems depth inside a game that reads as simple on the surface** —
which is a fair description of what Sandfall built. *Expedition 33* is a turn-based RPG that asks
for action-game reflexes on every incoming attack, and its parry and dodge windows are the thing
players talk about long after they stop talking about the story. The lineage is not hidden once
you know to look for it.

## A caveat Broche did not add

*Kingdom Hearts II* is the best entry in that series and one of the worst places to start it.
Twenty years of plot are wound through games across a shelf of platforms, several of which are
awkward to reach now — the series is at the point where *Kingdom Hearts IV* draws on characters
from games you can no longer straightforwardly buy.

So: a genuine recommendation, and a genuinely difficult one to act on. If Broche's description of
the combat is what drew you in, that part survives the plot confusion intact.

## Why we are covering a favourites list

Because Sandfall is French, small, and one game deep, and every public statement about what its
director values is a usable signal about what the studio builds next. Broche is not describing a
game he liked. He is describing a problem — a combat feel he has spent years trying to get back —
and *Expedition 33* is his first full attempt at solving it.
