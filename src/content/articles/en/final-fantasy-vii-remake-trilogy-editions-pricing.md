Start with the number that does not behave the way pricing normally behaves. The **Trilogy Edition**
of *Final Fantasy VII Revelation* costs **$84.99** and contains **three games**. The **Premium
Edition** costs **$99.99** and contains **one**.

That is not a rounding error or a launch-week glitch. It is the shape of the ladder Square Enix
published on **September 3, 2026**, alongside the **April 8, 2027** release date, and it means the
best-value purchase in the range is explicitly the one designed for people who have never touched
this trilogy.

## The four digital tiers

- **Trilogy Edition — $84.99.** *Final Fantasy VII Remake Intergrade*, *Final Fantasy VII Rebirth*
  and *Final Fantasy VII Revelation*, plus the pre-order bonus. Square Enix says this price is
  available **until April 7**, the day before launch.
- **Standard — $69.99.** *Revelation* and the pre-order bonus. Nothing else.
- **Premium — $99.99.** *Revelation*, the **Story Expansion Pass**, the pre-order bonus, and
  **24-hour early access**.
- **Premium Plus — $109.99.** The Premium contents plus further items.

A **Collector's Edition** exists on top of that.

## The oddity: three games cost less than one

Line the two ends up against each other and the incentive is unmistakable.

A newcomer pays **$84.99** and receives *Remake Intergrade*, *Rebirth* and *Revelation* — roughly two
hundred hours of game across three releases spanning 2020 to 2027.

Someone who bought *Remake* at launch in 2020 and *Rebirth* at launch in 2024 already owns two of
those three. Their entry point is the **$69.99** Standard edition, or **$99.99** if they want the
expansions. To get the expansion content they pay **$15 more than the person who is being handed the
entire trilogy**.

We are not going to pretend this is malicious. Bundles are discounted because bundles are sold to
people who would otherwise buy nothing, and a trilogy pack priced near a single deluxe edition is a
standard acquisition play. But it is worth naming the consequence honestly: **the pricing rewards
arriving late.** The player who supported the project for six years gets the worst per-hour rate in
the range, and there is no loyalty path — no upgrade price, no proof-of-ownership discount — that
corrects it.

If you own the first two games and were planning on the Premium tier, the only thing that softens
this is the save-data carry-over, which we cover in the trilogy status guide: an *Intergrade* save
grants the **Chocobo and Moogle** Summon Materia, and a *Rebirth* save grants **Phoenix** Materia
earlier than the game would otherwise give it. That is a reward for having played, not for having
paid, and it is worth something — but it is not $15 of storefront credit.

## What the Story Expansion Pass actually is

Two post-launch story add-ons. **The first is centred on Sephiroth. The second is centred on Vincent
and the Turks.** Square Enix has given a tentative window of **Winter 2027 to Spring 2028**.

Three things follow that you should weigh before paying $99.99 for a pass rather than $69.99 for a
game:

- **The window is tentative and it is a year out.** "Winter 2027 to Spring 2028" is a range, not a
  date, on content for a game that has not shipped.
- **No length, structure or price has been published for either add-on.** You are pre-paying for two
  unspecified quantities.
- **You cannot currently calculate the discount.** If the pass were sold separately at a known price
  the $30 premium would be assessable. It is not, so it is not.

The **24-hour early access** bundled into Premium is a real, if narrow, benefit. It also means the
first impressions circulating online will come from people who paid the top price, a day before
anyone on the Standard edition can check them.

## The Collector's Edition

Revealed at the same time. Square Enix has not published a contents breakdown detailed enough for us
to tell you whether it is worth its price, so we are not going to. Collector's editions are bought
for the objects in them; when the object list is public, compare it to the money and decide.

## The Switch 2 physical release is a Game-Key Card

This is the single most consequential detail in the whole announcement for anyone who buys physical.

The **Switch 2 physical edition of *Revelation* is a Game-Key Card**, and the listing puts the
download at **130 GB**.

What that means concretely:

- The cartridge does not hold the game. It holds an entitlement, and the game downloads.
- You need **130 GB of free storage** on the console or a card, and an internet connection to get it.
- The disc-shaped promise of a physical edition — install from the box, lend it, keep it working
  offline in ten years — applies only partially. The cartridge remains transferable, but it will
  always need the download.

None of this is unique to Square Enix and none of it is hidden; it is printed on the packaging. It
is, however, the thing most likely to surprise a buyer who assumed physical meant self-contained. If
you are on Switch 2 and the reason you wanted the box was independence from a download, this edition
does not give you that.

## What we would buy

- **New to the trilogy, or you own none of it:** the **Trilogy Edition at $84.99**, and it is not
  close. Three games at the price of one and a half, with a stated deadline of April 7.
- **You own *Remake* and *Rebirth* and just want the finale:** **Standard at $69.99**. Buy the
  expansions when they are announced with dates, scope and a price, and lose nothing by waiting — the
  first one is not expected before Winter 2027.
- **You want the 24-hour early access:** that is the honest reason to take Premium at $99.99, and it
  is a legitimate one. Take it for the early access, not because the pass is doing the persuading.
- **You are buying physical on Switch 2:** know that it is a **Game-Key Card at 130 GB** before you
  hand over the money, not after.

Nobody outside Square Enix has played this game. Every tier above Standard is a bet on content that
does not exist yet, placed seven months before the game does.
