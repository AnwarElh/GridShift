Three of the seven games this site tracks now have a Switch 2 story, and they are not the same story.
One is a platform where a game reviewed *best*. One is a first-ever native version. One is a
physical release that is not really physical. Here is what is actually confirmed, and what each
choice costs.

## What is confirmed, game by game

| Game | Switch 2 status | Date |
|---|---|---|
| **Onimusha: Way of the Sword** | Released, all four platforms simultaneously | 4 September 2026 |
| **The Witcher 3 — Remastered** | Native version, a first for Nintendo hardware | 29 September 2026 |
| **FF7 Revelation** | Confirmed, physical release is a Game-Key Card | 8 April 2027 |
| **FF7 Remake Intergrade / Rebirth** | Already there; Rebirth arrived 3 June 2026 | 2026 |
| **Clair Obscur: Expedition 33** | Rumoured only — no date, no confirmation | — |
| **GTA VI** | Not announced. PS5 and Xbox Series X\|S only | — |
| **The Blood of Dawnwalker** | Not announced | — |

## The one that scored highest there

This is the genuinely surprising entry. *Onimusha: Way of the Sword* launched on Switch 2, PS5, Xbox
Series X|S and PC on the same day, and when the aggregates settled the **Switch 2 version had the
highest Metacritic score of the four**: **88**, against 85 on PS5, 83 on Xbox Series X and 82 on PC.

Two things to hold at once about that number.

It is real — those are the published aggregates. But it is **not evidence that the Switch 2 version
runs best.** Different platforms draw different reviewer pools, and a game reviewed largely by
Nintendo-focused outlets is being scored by people writing for an audience with different
expectations. The spread is a fact about who reviewed what, not a benchmark.

What it does support is a narrower and more useful claim: **nobody found the Switch 2 version
compromised enough to mark it down for it.** For a linear, roughly twenty-hour action game on
Nintendo's hardware, that is the question most buyers actually have.

## The Witcher 3's first native Nintendo version

*The Witcher 3* reached the original Switch in 2019 as a cloud-assisted port with well-documented
compromises. The **29 September** release is **native**, and it is the first time.

That is the headline. The caveat is that **CD PROJEKT RED has published no performance information
for it** — no resolution, no frame-rate target, nothing per-platform. For a 2015 open-world game
being remastered with **enhanced path tracing** and running on a handheld, that is precisely the
gap you would want filled before pre-ordering.

Two further points specific to Switch 2 buyers:

- **There is no upgrade path.** The free upgrade covers PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Steam, GOG and the
  Epic Games Store. Owning the 2019 Switch version entitles you to nothing that CDPR has stated.
  Treat it as a new purchase.
- **Songs of the Past**, the paid 2027 expansion, is confirmed for Switch 2 alongside the others.

## The Game-Key Card question

*Final Fantasy VII Revelation*'s **Switch 2 physical edition is a Game-Key Card**, listed at
**130 GB**.

A Game-Key Card is a cartridge that authorises a download rather than carrying the game. Buying the
box gets you a licence and a 130 GB download. The practical consequences:

1. **Get the storage before launch day.** 130 GB is a large share of the console's internal storage,
   and an expansion card bought in advance costs less than one bought in a hurry.
2. **You need a connection that can pull 130 GB** in a timeframe you can live with.
3. **The cartridge is not a backup.** If you buy physical so the game exists independently of a
   storefront, this does not give you that.

None of that makes it the wrong version. It makes the physical edition a different purchase from the
one the box implies — worth knowing at the till rather than at home.

## What is not coming

Stating the absences plainly, because they are decisions rather than oversights:

- **GTA VI is PS5 and Xbox Series X|S only.** No Switch 2 version has been announced, and no PC
  version either.
- **The Blood of Dawnwalker** has announced no Switch 2 version.
- **Clair Obscur: Expedition 33** is **rumour only**. Leaker Nate the Hate reaffirmed in June 2026
  that it lands on Switch 2 "this year", but there is no eShop listing, no date and no confirmation
  from Sandfall or Kepler. Do not plan around it.

## What we would buy on Switch 2, and what we would not

**Buy there:** *Onimusha*. It is linear, roughly twenty hours, launched simultaneously, and no
reviewer marked the version down. It is the clearest case on the list.

**Buy there if portability is the point:** the *FF7* trilogy. *Remake Intergrade* and *Rebirth* are
already on the platform, and *Revelation* joins them in April — with the Game-Key Card caveat and
the storage planning that comes with it. Worth remembering that save-data bonuses read the platform
you play the finale on, so if the trilogy is going to live on Switch 2, put the earlier saves there.

**Wait:** *The Witcher 3 — Remastered*. Not because there is reason to doubt it, but because there
is no published performance information at all for the most demanding game on this list running on
the least powerful hardware. Twenty-four days is a short wait for that gap to close.
