There are seven months between now and *Final Fantasy VII Revelation*, which is exactly the amount of
time in which people make platform decisions badly. The list is wider than it has ever been for this
series, and two of the options carry conditions that are not obvious from a store page.

## The platform list, and what is actually decided

*Revelation* releases on **8 April 2027** on **PlayStation 5**, **Xbox Series X|S**, **Nintendo
Switch 2** and **Windows**. All four on the same day.

That is the whole of what has been confirmed about platforms. There are no announced performance
modes, no resolution or frame-rate targets, no PC system requirements, and no file sizes beyond the
one attached to the Switch 2 listing. Anyone telling you which version will run best is guessing.

What we can say is that the multiplatform work is not being improvised at the end. Director **Naoki
Hamaguchi** has described the team splitting into smaller groups assigned to optimise for each target
system, running that in parallel with the *Remake Intergrade* and *Rebirth* ports through 2026, and
has said every version is intended to retain gameplay and presentation parity.

## Windows: three storefronts, and the one that does something extra

The PC version launches on **Steam**, the **Epic Games Store** and the **Microsoft Store**
simultaneously. That is a first for this trilogy, which has previously staggered storefronts or
skipped them.

For most people the three are interchangeable and you should buy where your library already lives.
The exception is the Microsoft Store version, which is the only one that does something the others
do not — see below.

## Xbox and Play Anywhere

The Microsoft Store version supports **Play Anywhere cross-progression** with **Xbox Series X|S**.

If you expect to play on both a PC and an Xbox, that single fact settles your storefront choice, and
it is the only cross-platform save arrangement announced for this game. There is no announced
cross-progression between PlayStation and anything, between Switch 2 and anything, or between Steam
and a console.

So the practical rule is narrow but firm: **if you want one save across two devices, those two
devices are a Windows PC and an Xbox, bought through the Microsoft Store.** Every other combination
means starting again.

## Switch 2, and the 130 GB question

The **Switch 2 physical edition is a Game-Key Card**, listed at **130 GB**.

A Game-Key Card is a cartridge that authorises a download rather than carrying the game. Buying the
box gets you a licence and a 130 GB download; the storage is yours to find. Console-generation norms
being what they are this is not a scandal, but it does have consequences worth planning around:

- **You need the space before launch day, not on it.** 130 GB is a large fraction of a Switch 2's
  internal storage, and expansion cards are cheaper bought in advance than in a hurry.
- **You need a connection that can pull 130 GB** in a timeframe you can tolerate.
- **The cartridge is not a backup.** If you are buying physical because you want the game to exist
  independently of a storefront, a Game-Key Card does not give you that.

None of this makes the Switch 2 version the wrong choice. It makes the physical Switch 2 version a
different purchase from the one the box implies, and that is worth knowing at the till.

## Get your save files in order now

This is the part with a deadline you can actually act on, and it costs nothing.

Save data from the earlier games unlocks bonuses in *Revelation*:

- A **Remake Intergrade** save grants the **"Chocobo and Moogle" Summon Materia** set.
- A **Rebirth** save grants the **Phoenix Summon Materia**, available earlier than it otherwise
  would be.

Both are early-game conveniences rather than anything that changes the shape of a playthrough, so
treat this as free rather than essential. The catch is the one people trip over: the bonus reads save
data **on the platform you play Revelation on**. A *Rebirth* save sitting on a PS5 does nothing for a
copy of *Revelation* running on a Switch 2.

So if you are moving platforms for the finale — and after the 2026 ports a lot of people are — the
useful move is to decide the platform first, then make sure a save from the earlier games exists
there. *Remake Intergrade* and *Rebirth* both reached **Switch 2 and Xbox Series X|S during 2026**,
with *Rebirth* landing on Switch 2 on **3 June**, so for most destinations that is now possible.

If you own nothing yet and intend to buy in, the **Trilogy Edition at $84.99** puts all three games
on one platform for less than the Premium edition of *Revelation* alone. We have written that ladder
up separately; for the purposes of this guide, it also happens to be the cleanest way to guarantee
the save-data bonuses land where you need them.

## What we cannot tell you yet

To be explicit about the gaps, because seven months is long enough for confident nonsense to
circulate:

- **No performance modes, resolutions or frame-rate targets** on any platform.
- **No PC system requirements.**
- **No install sizes** other than the 130 GB on the Switch 2 Game-Key Card listing.
- **No review or preview embargo**, and no hands-on impressions from anybody outside Square Enix.
- **No cross-progression** beyond Microsoft Store ↔ Xbox Series.

We will revisit this piece when Square Enix publishes specifications, and we will keep saying
"unannounced" about everything else until it does.
