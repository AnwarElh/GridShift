**The Blood of Dawnwalker** ran a complete frame-rate news cycle in the fortnight before it launched,
and it is a useful one to understand, because the same claims are about to be made for three more
games on this site's slate.

## What happened, in order

1. Rebel Wolves announced that the console versions would be capped at **40 fps**.
2. The response was negative enough to be reported as a backlash.
3. On **24 August**, the studio said **"we heard your feedback"** and committed to a **60 fps mode
   via a day-one patch** — with the **Xbox Series S excluded**.
4. The game launched on **3 September**.

That is the whole sequence. Worth noting what it is not: the game did not ship at 40 and get patched
later. The commitment was made before launch and delivered at launch.

## Why 40 is a real number, not a fudge

The instinct is to read 40 fps as a studio splitting the difference between 30 and 60. It is not, and
the reason is worth knowing because it will come up again.

**40 fps only works on a 120 Hz display.** A 60 Hz screen divides evenly into 30 and 60, but not 40 —
you would get uneven frame pacing, which looks worse than a lower steady number. A 120 Hz screen
divides evenly into 40, and each frame is held for exactly three refreshes.

So a 40 fps mode is a genuine option, but a conditional one: it is a meaningful improvement over 30
**if you have a 120 Hz display**, and unavailable or worse if you do not. That conditionality is
precisely why announcing 40 as the ceiling reads badly — for a large share of the audience, it is not
an upgrade at all.

## The console that was left out

The 60 fps commitment **excludes the Xbox Series S**, and that is neither unusual nor a slight.

The Series S is a deliberately less powerful machine sold at a lower price. Microsoft requires
feature parity from developers, not performance parity, so a game offering 60 fps on Series X and not
on Series S is doing something entirely normal. The honest framing is that the Series S buys a lower
price with a lower ceiling, which was the deal from the start.

If you own one, the practical advice is unchanged: read the per-console line, not the headline.

## How to check what you are actually getting

Four things settle it, and only one of them is on the box:

1. **Is your display 120 Hz?** If not, a 40 fps mode is not available to you in any useful sense.
   Check the TV's specification, not the console's.
2. **Which console, specifically?** Series X and Series S are different products with different
   performance ceilings, and announcements increasingly say so.
3. **Is the mode in the shipped game or in a patch?** Dawnwalker's arrived at launch. That is not
   guaranteed elsewhere.
4. **Has the publisher published anything at all?** Frequently the answer is no, and no number is
   different from a bad number.

## What has not been published for the games still to come

This is where the Dawnwalker episode becomes useful rather than historical. Three games on this
site's slate have published **nothing** about performance:

- **The Witcher 3 — Remastered** (29 September). No resolution, no frame-rate target, no per-platform
  breakdown — including for the first-ever **native Switch 2** version, which is the most demanding
  case on the list running on the least powerful hardware.
- **Grand Theft Auto VI** (19 November). No performance modes announced for PS5 or Xbox Series X|S.
- **FF7 Revelation** (8 April 2027). No targets on any of its four platforms.

For all three, the honest position is the same: nobody outside those studios knows, and a
pre-order made on an assumption about frame rate is a pre-order made on nothing.

The one thing Dawnwalker demonstrated is that these numbers are not always final. A cap announced in
August became a 60 fps mode by launch because enough people said the cap was not good enough. That is
worth remembering the next time a number lands badly — and worth not relying on, because it does not
always happen.
