HEAT launched in May 2026 with full cross-platform play *and* progression across PC — both the
Wargaming Game Center and Steam — plus Steam Deck, PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S and NVIDIA GeForce
NOW. That list is worth reading carefully, because it includes a cloud option, which is rarer than
the rest.

## The engine is new, and that matters here

HEAT is built on a new proprietary engine developed in-house by Wargaming rather than the one running
World of Tanks. The stated aim is modern visuals with responsive performance across a wide hardware
range. Practically, that means the usual assumption — that a Wargaming title will run on anything —
should be re-tested rather than assumed on older machines.

## Where GeForce NOW earns its place

A cloud option on a fast, team-based shooter is a real trade: you gain hardware independence and lose
some input latency. For a Marksman-role player working at range, that trade is more tolerable than it
is for an Assault player whose job is to close distance and finish openings before the enemy resets.
If you intend to play Assault primarily, prefer local hardware.

## Steam Deck

Supported at launch. The three-role structure helps here — Defender play asks less of your reaction
time than Assault does, and the Deck is a reasonable place to hold a line.

## Account, not platform

Progression follows the account across all of the above. There is no penalty for starting on console
and moving to PC later, which removes the main reason to agonise over the first install.
