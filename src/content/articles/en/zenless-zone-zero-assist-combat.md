Most players hit a wall somewhere in the third difficulty tier, conclude their agents are
under-levelled, and go and farm. Levels are usually not the problem. The problem is that they are
playing ZZZ as a rotation game — swap on cooldown, dump damage, repeat — when it is built as a
reaction game.

## The three interrupts, in the order you will need them

**Dodge Counter.** Dodge at the moment an attack lands and the follow-up attack becomes a counter.
This is the one the tutorial teaches and the one most players over-use, because dodging early feels
safe. It is not: an early dodge is just a dodge, and you give up the counter.

**Defensive Assist.** When an enemy is about to land a heavy attack, the game flags it and you can
bring in an off-field agent to meet it. They parry, they take the field, and the swap costs you
nothing — no cooldown, no animation lock. This is the single most under-used mechanic in the game.
Players treat incoming heavies as something to avoid; they are the cheapest character swap you have.

**Quick Assist.** When the on-field agent is knocked back, an off-field agent can be launched in
immediately. Getting hit is not supposed to end your sequence.

Learn them in that order, but understand that Defensive Assist is the one that changes how the game
feels.

## Daze is the real damage window

Every enemy has a Daze bar. Fill it and the enemy is stunned, takes substantially increased damage,
and your team can fire a Chain Attack — each agent in sequence, chosen by you.

This reframes what a team is for. Your Stun-specialty agent is not a support; they are the one
opening the window that everyone else's damage goes through. A team that stacks three damage dealers
and no Stun agent does more damage per second on paper and less damage per fight in practice, because
it never gets the multiplier.

## Anomaly is the stat nobody tells you about

Each agent has an attribute — Physical, Fire, Ice, Electric or Ether. Apply enough of one and you
trigger an Anomaly: burn, shatter, shock, assault or corruption. Anomaly damage scales off Anomaly
Mastery and Anomaly Proficiency, not off Attack.

Here is the failure mode: a new player builds their Anomaly-specialty agent for Attack and Crit
because those are the stats every action game has taught them to want, and the character's damage
flatlines. If your agent's specialty is Anomaly, the substats you keep on Drive Discs are the mastery
ones. This is not a marginal optimisation — it is the difference between a working character and a
broken one.

## Building a team that can answer, not just attack

A functional three-agent team is roughly: one main damage dealer, one Stun agent to open the window,
one support or Anomaly agent to fill it. Same-faction and same-attribute bonuses exist and are real,
but they are a tiebreaker, not a starting point.

The test to apply is not "does this team do damage". It is "when the enemy throws a heavy attack, do
I have someone I want to bring in?" If the answer is no, you have three attackers and no answers.

## The habit that separates the tiers

Watch the enemy, not your cooldowns. ZZZ signals almost everything — heavy attacks flash, Daze bars
fill visibly, the assist prompt is unmissable once you are looking for it. Players who clear the
higher tiers are not inputting more; they are inputting later, in response to something.

Spend one session deliberately never swapping on cooldown — only on a Defensive Assist, a Quick
Assist or a Chain Attack. It will feel slower for about ten minutes and then the game will click.
