Onimusha: Way of the Sword is out today on PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch 2 and PC
via Steam and Epic. It is the first mainline entry in the series since *Dawn of Dreams* in 2006 — a
gap of twenty years — and it arrives three weeks ahead of the date Capcom originally gave it.

## The date moved forward, not back

Capcom announced on July 1 that the game would ship on September 4 rather than September 25. Publishers
move release dates all the time; they almost never move them earlier. The July announcement went out
through Capcom's investor relations channel as well as the usual press route, which is the tell: a
date pulled forward by three weeks in an autumn this crowded is a scheduling decision made by people
looking at a calendar of other people's launches, not a marketing beat.

Whatever the reasoning, the practical effect is that a Capcom action game landed the day after
The Blood of Dawnwalker and well clear of November.

## Twenty years, and a face that cost two of them

The protagonist is **Miyamoto Musashi**, and his likeness is **Toshiro Mifune's**. Capcom has said
securing the rights to use the actor's likeness took two years on its own; development began in 2020,
on RE Engine, under director Satoru Nihei and producer Akihito Kadowaki.

The game is not a continuation of the previous Onimusha titles and is not connected to the Netflix
series. It is set in a dark-fantasy Edo-era Kyoto disfigured by clouds of "Malice", and Capcom worked
with officials at real Japanese temples to get the period detail right on at least one major
location.

## What it actually is

Largely linear, roughly twenty hours, with open areas and side quests threaded through it. Explicitly
not an open world and explicitly not a Soulslike — Nihei said as much to GameSpot back in June 2025,
and the shipped game holds to it.

Combat is parry-led: deflect projectiles, hold a prolonged parry to steer an enemy into falling where
you want, drain stamina until a **Break Issen** execution opens up. A sentient gauntlet eats souls in
three colours — yellow for health, red for upgrades, blue for the high-damage **Oni Armaments**. It
is gory, and unapologetically so.

## The reviews are in, and the Switch 2 version won

The embargo lifted on August 31. Metacritic has it in "generally favorable" territory across all four
platforms, with an unusual distribution: **88 on Switch 2, 85 on PS5, 83 on Xbox Series X, 82 on PC**.
OpenCritic has 94% of critics recommending it.

A Switch 2 version topping the list while the PC version sits at the bottom is not how this normally
goes, and it is worth watching whether that reflects the game or the reviewers. GamesRadar's line —
"it's no Sekiro, but goofiness, accessibility, and tight combat help this rough diamond shine" — sits
opposite VGC's, which called it a series reborn with great fights but "too many wrong modern lessons".
Polygon's preview had already framed it as a welcome break from the Soulslike.
