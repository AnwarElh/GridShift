The single most common way to bounce off Once Human is to spend a first scenario building something
beautiful, watch it disappear at the reset, and conclude the game wasted your time. It did not —
but only if you knew what to pack.

## The two clocks you are playing against

Every scenario runs for a set number of weeks with its own phases: early build-up, mid-season
progression, then the closing phase. That is the first clock, and it is visible in the interface.

The second clock is your own account's meta-progression, and it does not reset. Understanding that
these are separate is the whole mental shift the game asks for. Inside a scenario, you are playing a
survival game. Across scenarios, you are playing a collection game — and the collection is knowledge,
not power.

## What survives a scenario, precisely

**Kept:** your blueprints — the formulas for weapons, gear and mods you have acquired — along with
your cosmetics, your account-level unlocks, and whatever you have moved into Eternaland.

**Lost:** your character level, your territory and everything built on it, your inventory, your gear
as equipped, and your progress through the season's technology tree.

The consequence is worth stating plainly: **an hour spent acquiring a blueprint is worth more than
an hour spent upgrading a weapon.** The weapon is gone in a fortnight. The formula is not, and next
scenario it is available from the moment you can afford the materials.

Players who understand this play the late season entirely differently. They stop optimising gear they
are about to lose and start hunting the things that outlive it.

## Eternaland is a suitcase, not a warehouse

Eternaland is your persistent private space, and you can carry a limited quantity of materials and
constructions into it before a scenario closes. The limit is the point — it is not a place to store
your base, it is a place to choose from your base.

The useful packing rule is to prioritise by *scarcity in the early game*, not by value. High-tier
materials you will grind again anyway are a poor use of the allowance. The things worth carrying are
whatever was tedious in the first ten hours of the last scenario: early-tier crafting components,
starter-grade resources you always run short of, and the constructions you would otherwise rebuild by
hand.

## The last week: a checklist

- **Finish blueprint acquisition first.** Anything that awards a formula outranks anything that
  awards materials.
- **Spend seasonal currencies to zero.** They do not travel.
- **Complete the scenario's own milestone rewards** — many pay out in things that do carry.
- **Pack Eternaland deliberately**, not on the final day in a hurry.
- **Take screenshots of your base layout.** Unromantic and genuinely useful: rebuilding a design you
  liked is much faster from a picture than from memory.

## Choosing the next scenario

Scenarios differ in ruleset — PvE and PvP variants, different resource pressures, different map
behaviour — and the right choice depends on what you want to change, not on what is newest.

The advice that holds for most players: do your second scenario in the same ruleset as your first.
You will move through the early game two or three times faster with blueprints in hand, and that
acceleration is the reward the structure was designed to give you. Feeling it once is what makes the
reset stop feeling like a loss.
