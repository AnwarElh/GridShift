**Travis Willingham** takes over as the English voice of **Sephiroth** in *Final Fantasy VII
Revelation*, replacing **Tyler Hoechlin**, who played the role in *Remake* (2020) and *Rebirth*
(2024).

Square Enix's stated reason is that **Hoechlin was unavailable during Revelation's production**. The
**Japanese performance is unchanged**: **Toshiyuki Morikawa** returns, as he has since 1997's
supporting media and throughout the remake trilogy.

## Why this one landed harder than most recasts

Recasting happens constantly and rarely travels beyond a patch note. This one did, for a reason worth
naming: *Revelation* is the game the whole trilogy has been building toward, and Sephiroth is the
figure it builds toward. Kotaku's June headline — *"Fans Are Sad And Confused After Discovering Final
Fantasy 7 Revelation Will Change The Actor Who Plays Sephiroth"* — captures the shape of the reaction
accurately: less anger than deflation.

The confusion is the more interesting half. A recast between the second and third parts of a
continuous story invites the assumption that something went wrong. Square Enix's explanation is that
nothing did — an actor was not available across the window the work required. That is the ordinary
answer, and it is the one on the record.

## What is actually known

The complete list, because this is a story where inference has outpaced fact:

- **Travis Willingham voices Sephiroth in English** in *Revelation*.
- **Tyler Hoechlin was unavailable** during the game's production. That is the stated reason.
- **Toshiyuki Morikawa returns** in Japanese.
- *Revelation* releases **8 April 2027** on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch 2 and Windows.

What has **not** been said: whether any other roles are recast, whether previously recorded lines are
being reused anywhere, and whether the change affects any other language track. Nobody outside Square
Enix has heard the new performance in context.

## The wider point about the finale's timing

*Revelation* has had the **shortest production cycle of the three games** — roughly three years.
Reporting by **Jason Schreier for Bloomberg** in June attributes that to Square Enix keeping the
**vast majority of the core team** together across *Remake*, *Rebirth* and the finale, rather than
disbanding staff between projects as has happened elsewhere in the company.

That continuity is the context this story sits inside. A project that deliberately held its
development team together for seven years lost a voice actor to a scheduling conflict — which is a
reminder that performance capture and voice work run on availability calendars that a studio's own
staffing decisions cannot control.

The date was confirmed at Sony's State of Play on 3 September, along with the edition line-up and
pre-orders. We have covered both separately.
