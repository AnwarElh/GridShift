*The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt — Remastered* is a free upgrade arriving **September 29, 2026**, and the
feature list CD PROJEKT RED published is longer than a remaster usually justifies. Most of it is
visual. A couple of items are not, and those are the ones worth reading carefully, because they
change a game that a very large number of people have already finished two or three times.

## What is confirmed, and where it comes from

Everything below comes from **CD PROJEKT RED's own announcement material**. That is an important
caveat and it applies to every line of this guide: these are the studio's claims about its own
product, published to sell an upgrade, and none of them has been independently tested. *Remastered*
is being co-developed with **Yigsoft**, a team CDPR says already has a track record on the game.

We will revisit this after release with a version that says what is actually true.

## The visual work

Four items, all straightforwardly presentational:

- **Enhanced path tracing.** *The Witcher 3* already received a ray-traced overhaul in its 2022
  next-gen update; this is described as an enhancement of path tracing specifically, which is the
  more expensive and more complete lighting technique.
- **Updated dynamic lighting.**
- **Higher-resolution textures.**
- **Improved environmental foliage across all regions.** The phrase "across all regions" is doing
  real work — Velen, Skellige, Toussaint and Novigrad have distinct vegetation, and foliage is the
  single most visible thing in a game people mostly experience while riding through countryside.

None of this changes what you do. It changes what it looks like while you do it, which for this
particular game is not a trivial category.

## The combat changes

Four named changes, and this is where a remaster starts editing rather than polishing:

- **Refined targeting.** *The Witcher 3*'s target acquisition is the single most-complained-about
  part of its combat, and has been since 2015. "Refined" is vague, but the area is correctly chosen.
- **New gap-closing animations.** Geralt's lunges into range are among the game's most-mocked
  animations. New ones would materially change the moment-to-moment rhythm of a fight.
- **Dynamic camera angles.**
- **Sign-based finishers.** New execution animations tied to the Signs — the first time the Signs
  have had finishers of their own.

Combat is the part of *The Witcher 3* that its own defenders concede least. Changing it is the
riskiest thing on this list and the most interesting.

## Traversal, mounts and monsters

- **Improved traversal and movement.** Geralt's movement — specifically his weight and the delay
  between input and step — is the other perennial complaint. Alongside "refined targeting", this
  suggests CDPR went after the two things people actually name.
- **New witcher Sign effects.** Presentational, paired with the finishers above.
- **Revamped mount handling.** Roach is a running joke with a decade of momentum behind it.
- **Enhanced monster behaviours.** The most ambiguous item on the list. It could mean better AI
  routines in combat, or it could mean richer idle behaviour in the world. CDPR has not said which,
  and the difference is large.

## The two that matter most: skill tree and transmog

**The skill tree revamp** is the biggest single item here and the one CDPR has said least about.
*The Witcher 3*'s skill system has a well-known structural problem: a limited number of active
slots forces you to leave most of what you unlock switched off, which makes levelling feel like it
is subtracting choices rather than adding them. A "revamp" could mean anything from rebalancing to
a rebuild. Until CDPR is specific, treat it as the item with the widest range of possible outcomes —
and the one most likely to change how a replay actually feels.

**Transmogrification** lets you separate how gear looks from what it does. In a game with a heavily
signposted armour progression and a strong visual identity, that is the quality-of-life feature the
modding community built for itself years ago, arriving officially. It is small, it is not
mechanical, and it is the one people will notice within ten minutes of loading a save.

## The expanded photo mode

Listed, confirmed, and not detailed further. The base game received a photo mode in the 2022 update;
this is an expansion of it.

## What has not been said

The gaps, stated plainly so nobody fills them in with assumptions:

- **No performance targets.** No resolutions, no frame-rate modes, no per-platform breakdown — which
  matters most for the new native Switch 2 version, where the questions are hardest.
- **No detail on the skill tree revamp**, the single most consequential item on the list.
- **No clarification of "enhanced monster behaviours".**
- **No save compatibility statement.** Whether an existing playthrough carries into *Remastered* has
  not been addressed, and with a skill tree revamp in the mix it is a genuine question.
- **No PC system requirements** for the path-traced configuration.
- **No review embargo, and no hands-on impressions from anyone outside the studios involved.**

Twenty-five days is not long. We will update this guide as CDPR fills the gaps, and replace the
studio's adjectives with our own once we have played it.
