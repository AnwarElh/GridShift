For most games, on most days, which PC storefront you buy on does not matter. You install a launcher
you already have and forget about it.

Three of the games this site covers make that assumption wrong in different ways. Here is where each
is sold, and the two cases where the choice has consequences you cannot undo later.

## Where each game is actually sold

| Game | Steam | GOG | Epic | Microsoft Store | Battle.net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Witcher 3 — Remastered** | yes | yes | yes | yes (Xbox on PC) | **yes, from 29 Sept** |
| **FF7 Revelation** | yes | — | yes | **yes** | — |
| **The Blood of Dawnwalker** | yes | yes | — | yes (Xbox on PC) | — |

*The Witcher 3* reaching five storefronts is unusual, and Battle.net is the new one — a platform that
has sold essentially only Blizzard's own games for nearly thirty years.

## The one case where the storefront changes what you get

**Final Fantasy VII Revelation.** It launches on Steam, the Epic Games Store and the Microsoft Store
**simultaneously** — a first for the series, which has previously staggered or skipped storefronts.

The Microsoft Store version is the only one that does something the others do not: it supports
**Play Anywhere cross-progression with Xbox Series X|S**.

That single fact settles the choice for one group of people. **If you expect to play on both a
Windows PC and an Xbox, buy on the Microsoft Store.** It is the only cross-platform save arrangement
announced for this game — there is none between PlayStation and anything, none between Switch 2 and
anything, and none between Steam and a console.

Every other combination means starting again on the second device.

This compounds with something else worth knowing: **save-data bonuses read the platform you play
Revelation on**. A *Rebirth* save on a PS5 does nothing for a copy of *Revelation* running on PC. So
the storefront decision and the "where does my trilogy live" decision are the same decision, and it
is best made once.

## DRM-free, and who still offers it

**GOG** sells DRM-free builds — the installer works without a running client, and the game keeps
working if the storefront does not.

Two of the three games here are on GOG: *The Witcher 3* (CD PROJEKT's own platform) and *The Blood of
Dawnwalker*. *FF7 Revelation* is not.

If that matters to you, it is the kind of thing that only matters at purchase. You cannot convert a
Steam licence into a GOG one afterwards.

## Free upgrades follow the storefront

This is the trap most likely to cost someone money this month.

*The Witcher 3 — Remastered* is a **free upgrade** on **PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Steam, GOG and the
Epic Games Store**. It follows the copy you already own, on the storefront you already own it on.

What that means in practice:

- **Buying it again on Battle.net gets you nothing extra.** Battle.net is a place to buy the game,
  not an upgrade path. If you own it on Steam, you already have the remaster coming.
- **The 2019 Switch version confers nothing** toward the native Switch 2 release, as far as CDPR has
  stated.
- **PS4 and Xbox One owners are not specifically addressed** by CDPR's wording, which covers PS5 and
  Xbox Series X|S. That is a gap rather than a refusal; we would not assume either way.

## A decision rule that fits in one line

For nearly everything: **buy where your library already lives.** Launcher fragmentation is a real
cost and storefront-hopping for a few euros is rarely worth it.

The exceptions, in full:

1. **Playing on PC *and* Xbox?** Microsoft Store, for Play Anywhere. This is the only one that is
   genuinely irreversible.
2. **Want the game to outlive the storefront?** GOG, where available.
3. **Already own The Witcher 3 anywhere qualifying?** Buy nothing. The upgrade is free.

We have covered each game's full platform picture, and the Witcher upgrade's eligibility rules,
separately.
