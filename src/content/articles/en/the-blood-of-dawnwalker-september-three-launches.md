Three of the games this site covers ship inside a single month, and two of them landed on
consecutive days. The clustering is worth laying out, because it was not inevitable — at least one
of these dates was moved deliberately to produce it.

## The three dates

| Game | Date | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **The Blood of Dawnwalker** | 3 September | Rebel Wolves' first game; went gold mid-July |
| **Onimusha: Way of the Sword** | 4 September | Moved up three weeks from 25 September |
| **The Witcher 3 — Remastered** | 29 September | Free upgrade for existing owners |

## Capcom moved, and moved forward

The unusual decision is **Onimusha**. Capcom announced on **1 July** that it was bringing the game
forward from **25 September to 4 September** — three weeks earlier, across all platforms.

Pulling a release forward is rarer than pushing it back, and it is worth being careful about why.
Capcom stated the change through its investor relations channel; it did not publish a reason. Any
explanation beyond "the date moved" is inference, and we are not going to supply one.

What it produced, though, is factual: the first mainline *Onimusha* in twenty years launched **one
day after** a debut RPG from a new studio, into the same week, for roughly the same audience.

## Dawnwalker did not move, and was ready early

**The Blood of Dawnwalker** had its date confirmed in **April** and **went gold in mid-July** —
about seven weeks before release. It launched on 3 September as scheduled, on PS5, Xbox Series X|S
and PC.

Its one late change was not the date but the specification: after the announced 40 fps cap on
consoles drew a negative response, Rebel Wolves committed on **24 August** to a **60 fps mode via
day-one patch**, saying "we heard your feedback".

## The Witcher 3 is the odd one out, because it is free

*The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt — Remastered* arrives on **29 September**, and it is not competing for the
same money. For anyone who already owns the game on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Steam, GOG or the Epic
Games Store, it is a **free upgrade**.

That changes what a crowded month means. Two of these releases are purchases; the third is a
sixty-five-million-owner back catalogue reactivating itself at no cost, three weeks after the other
two have taken people's evenings.

It also arrives on **Nintendo Switch 2** natively for the first time — the same platform where
*Onimusha* posted its highest review scores.

## What it looks like from the reader's side

Both September purchases are long games. *Dawnwalker* runs **50 to 70 hours**; *Onimusha* is
**around 20**, and deliberately linear. They are not competing for the same evening in the way two
hundred-hour open worlds would.

Then, on the 29th, a game a great many people already own becomes substantially different for free —
with **Hearts of Stone** and **Blood and Wine** now included for every owner, effective since the
gamescom announcement.

October's picture is not yet clear. What is clear is that the month's three releases are a debut, a
twenty-year revival and an eleven-year-old game's second life, which is an unusually wide spread for
four weeks.
