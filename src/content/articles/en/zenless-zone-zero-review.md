ZZZ launched in July 2024 into an argument about a mode that no longer exists. The TV-grid navigation
that dominated its first month has been steadily reduced and replaced; the combat that was buried
under it is now the whole game. That is a rare thing for a live-service studio to do, and it is the
main reason the score is where it is.

## The combat is the argument

Three agents, one on the field, and a set of interrupts that let you swap between them without ever
letting the fight breathe. A perfect dodge opens a Dodge Counter. An incoming heavy attack can be met
with a Defensive Assist that parries it and puts a different character on the field mid-animation.
Fill an enemy's Daze bar and the team fires a Chain Attack in sequence.

The effect is that switching characters is not a rotation you memorise but a response to what the
enemy is doing. Most team-based action games make the swap a cooldown; ZZZ makes it a defensive tool.
Two years on, nothing else in the genre reads quite like it — and it is why the game survives having
so little else.

## Anomaly is the system worth learning

Each agent has an attribute — Physical, Fire, Ice, Electric or Ether — and applying enough of one
triggers an Anomaly: burn, shatter, shock, assault, corruption. Anomaly damage scales off a stat most
new players ignore for twenty hours, and an Anomaly-built team clears content that a raw-damage team
stalls on.

This is where ZZZ's depth actually lives, and the game does a poor job of saying so. The tutorial
teaches you to dodge and to chain. It does not teach you that the reason your damage fell off a cliff
at the third difficulty tier is that you built for attack on a character who wanted mastery.

## Where it runs out

New Eridu is a hub. You walk a street, talk to a vendor, take a commission, and load into a corridor.
There is no exploration layer, no map to open, no traversal to master. Compared with its own
stablemates that is a deliberate scope choice, and for a game you can play in twenty-minute sessions
it is defensible — but it means the only reason to keep the client installed is combat, and combat
is gated.

Battery Charge regenerates one per six minutes to a cap of 240, and every progression material comes
out of it. Half an hour of purposeful play a day is the honest figure. Drive Discs — the equivalent
of Genshin's artifacts, with the same random main stats, random substats and random upgrade rolls —
consume most of it, with the same miserable hit rate.

## What 3.1 got right

Enigma of the Labyrinth: Operation Bagel, added at the anniversary, is permanent rather than
seasonal. That distinction matters more than its content does. A live-service game whose good modes
expire teaches players not to invest in learning them; a permanent mode is somewhere your account
progress compounds. More of this, and the "nothing to do" complaint stops being true.

## Who it is for

If you want the best forty minutes of action combat available on a phone, a PS5 and a PC with the
same save, start now — the 3.x cycle is a clean entry point and the six-week rhythm makes it easy to
plan around. If what you want from a HoYoverse game is a world to disappear into, this is not it, and
three versions of evidence say it is not trying to be.

8.0 is a high score for a game with one great system and not much around it. That is exactly what
this is.
