World of Tanks: HEAT launched on 26 May 2026 with two significant risks attached. The first was
technical: a new proprietary engine, built in-house rather than the one running World of Tanks, aimed
at modern visuals with responsive performance across a wide hardware range. The second was
structural: no tech tree, and a roster of eight Agents across three roles, with fifteen tanks.

Three months on, it is fair to say the first risk is settled and the second is not.

## The engine held

The new engine shipped with support across PC — through both the Wargaming Game Center and Steam —
plus Steam Deck, PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S and NVIDIA GeForce NOW, with cross-platform play and
progression across all of them. A cloud target on a fast, team-based shooter is the hardest case on
that list, and it is on the list.

That is a wide surface to support at launch, and supporting it on a first-generation engine is not
the usual outcome. Whatever else is arguable about HEAT, the technical foundation is not the thing
holding it back.

## The arithmetic of eight

A role-based shooter earns its long tail from composition. The interesting decisions happen before
the shooting: what the enemy has picked, what counters it, what your team is missing. That decision
space grows with the roster, and it grows fast — every Agent added multiplies against every Agent
already there.

Eight Agents across three roles, with three of them the introductory picks — Chopper for Defender,
Kent for Assault, Hound for Marksman — is a small number to build that layer on. It is not a
criticism of the individual designs, which are clear and distinct. It is arithmetic. The number of
compositions a regular player will see is small enough to exhaust.

## Why this is the number to watch

Wargaming has been explicit that HEAT drops the vehicle progression ladder. That decision removes the
thing that traditionally kept players installed — a tree to climb — and replaces it with the
proposition that the moment-to-moment game is good enough to return to on its own merits.

For that to hold over a year rather than a season, the composition layer has to keep changing. In a
game without a tech tree, the Agent roster is the only content axis that does that. Fifteen tanks
sitting inside three roles do not create new matches; new Agents do.

## What would settle it

A cadence. Not a single Agent as a headline, but a visible, sustained rate of addition — the thing
the genre's successful examples established early and the unsuccessful ones promised. Wargaming has
not published one.

Until it does, HEAT's technical achievement is complete and its design case is open. Both are true at
once, and the second is the one worth checking back on.
