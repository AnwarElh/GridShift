*Updated September 4, 2026, after Sony's State of Play dated the finale.*

**Final Fantasy VII Revelation** arrives on **April 8, 2027**, and closes a project that has been
running since 2020. If you are coming back to it after a couple of years away — or deciding whether
to start — the useful questions are not about the finale's trailer. They are about what the first two
games committed to, what changed around them in 2026, and whether the save files on your old console
are worth anything.

## The shape of the project, and when it became a trilogy

Square Enix confirmed in **2015**, as *Remake* went into production, that the project would be split
across multiple games. It did not say how many. That number arrived in **June 2022**, when the studio
confirmed it would be a **trilogy** — which is to say the audience spent seven years not knowing
where the thing was going to end.

- **Final Fantasy VII Remake** — 2020.
- **Final Fantasy VII Rebirth** — 2024.
- **Final Fantasy VII Revelation** — April 8, 2027.

All three reimagine the 1997 PlayStation original rather than remastering it. That distinction is the
whole reason a single 1997 game needs three modern ones.

## Reunion, bonds, and now choice

Each part has a stated driving motif, and they are not marketing decoration — they describe fairly
accurately what each game is structurally about.

- ***Remake*: reunion.** A single city, a tight cast, characters converging.
- ***Rebirth*: bonds.** The road trip, the relationships, the affinity systems that made who you were
  travelling with a mechanical fact and not just a story one.
- ***Revelation*: choice and resolve.** Square Enix's stated theme for the finale.

The title itself was kept secret from all but a handful of the team until announcement, specifically
to prevent leaks — a detail that tells you something about how tightly the ending has been held.

## 2026 was the year it stopped being a PlayStation trilogy

For most of its life this was a PS5-and-Windows project. That ended over the course of **2026**.

**Final Fantasy VII Remake Intergrade** and **Final Fantasy VII Rebirth** both reached **Nintendo
Switch 2 and Xbox Series X|S** during the year, with *Rebirth* landing on Switch 2 on **June 3,
2026**. By the time *Revelation* ships in April 2027, all three games will be available on all four
platforms.

The practical effect is that "start the trilogy now" no longer requires owning a specific console.
The catch-up path exists everywhere the finale does.

## What your old saves are worth

Two confirmed carry-over bonuses, and they are the reason not to delete anything:

- **A *Final Fantasy VII Remake Intergrade* save** grants the **"Chocobo and Moogle" Summon Materia**
  in *Revelation*.
- **A *Final Fantasy VII Rebirth* save** grants the **Phoenix Summon Materia**, available earlier in
  the game than it otherwise would be.

Two notes on this before you plan around it. First, these are summon Materia, not story content —
useful, not decisive. Second, Square Enix has not published the mechanics of how a save is detected
across the four platforms, and with the trilogy now spread over PS5, Xbox Series, Switch 2 and three
separate Windows storefronts, "which save on which machine" is a real question that has not been
answered. If it matters to you, keep the saves and wait for the detail rather than assuming a
platform transfer exists.

## What Revelation actually has to adapt

*Revelation* picks up **directly from the end of *Rebirth*** and covers the remainder of the 1997
game: from the party's arrival at the **Great Glacier** through to the final confrontation with
Sephiroth at the **Northern Crater**.

Two locations absent from *Rebirth* return and have been confirmed as prominent: **Wutai** and
**Rocket Town**. **Underwater traversal by submarine** is in. So is a new Chocobo mechanic, which
Square Enix has been explicit is *not* the original's Chocobo breeding.

If you played the 1997 game, that list will tell you roughly how much ground the finale is covering.
If you did not, the short version is: this is the part of the story where the map opens up and the
plot stops circling.

## What carries over mechanically

**Combat.** *Revelation* introduces the **FITS system**, designed by combat director **Teruki Endo**,
which lets each party member change play style according to *Final Fantasy* **jobs**. Director Naoki
Hamaguchi has framed it as an extension of *Rebirth*'s combat rather than a rebuild — the stated
concern being to add depth without making the system convoluted. If you learned *Rebirth*'s combat,
you are not starting again.

**Minigames.** Hamaguchi has talked openly about resolving what he called the minigame issue, which
is a polite way of referring to one of *Rebirth*'s most divisive features. The announced approach has
two halves: returning minigames such as **Queen's Blood** come back in **enhanced** forms, and
material from later in the original — the **Great Glacier snowboarding**, for instance — has been
reimagined to connect to the narrative rather than sit beside it.

Whether that resolves anything is a question for April 2027. It is at least a stated position rather
than a silence.

## The things that changed behind the scenes

Three items worth knowing, reported rather than announced:

**The production cycle is the shortest of the three.** *Revelation* has taken roughly three years.
**Jason Schreier, writing for Bloomberg on June 12, 2026**, attributes this to Square Enix keeping the
**vast majority of the core team** together across all three games, rather than disbanding staff
between projects as has happened elsewhere in the company. Hamaguchi said in April 2026 that he had
already played the game to completion around **forty times**.

**Rebirth underperformed internally.** Square Enix was disappointed with *Rebirth*'s sales on PS5 and
Windows. Hamaguchi has publicly addressed the concern that this would cost the finale resources,
saying it would be a proper, high-quality send-off and that much of it was already playable at the
time he spoke. That is the developer's position, offered here as the developer's position.

**Sephiroth has a new English voice.** **Travis Willingham replaces Tyler Hoechlin**, who was
unavailable during *Revelation*'s production. The Japanese cast is unchanged: **Toshiyuki Morikawa**
returns.

## If you are starting from zero

Play them in order. *Remake Intergrade* first, then *Rebirth*, then the finale. The trilogy is built
as one continuous story with mechanical and narrative dependencies running forward through it, and
*Revelation* begins on the exact frame *Rebirth* ended on.

You have until April 8, 2027, which is roughly seven months to get through two games that will take
most people well over a hundred hours combined. That is comfortable, but it is not infinite.
