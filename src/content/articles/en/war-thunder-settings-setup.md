War Thunder's settings menu is enormous and almost none of it matters. Six things do, and two of
them are the difference between spotting a hull-down tank at 800 metres and being shot by it.

## Two graphics settings that are competitive, not cosmetic

**Grass and vegetation rendering distance.** Foliage is drawn client-side and only within a set
distance, which means a tank sitting in grass at long range may be fully visible on your screen and
completely hidden on someone else's — and vice versa. Setting vegetation distance low does not make
you invisible to others, but it does stop you from being the player who cannot see through their own
bushes. This is the single most consequential graphics option in the game.

**Terrain and object rendering quality.** Distant terrain detail changes what silhouettes resolve
against a ridge. Low settings flatten the ground into shapes you can read; very high settings make it
prettier and busier. In Realistic Battles, readable beats pretty.

Everything else in that menu — shadows, cloud quality, SSAO, water — is taste. Set it to whatever
your machine holds comfortably.

## Turn the frame rate up before you turn the quality up

Ground Realistic is a game of small movements at distance, and a stable frame rate makes tracking a
moving target across a treeline genuinely easier. Disable vertical sync unless you have screen
tearing you cannot tolerate, and accept a lower preset to hold a steady number. On current-generation
consoles the game runs at 4K and 60 fps, which is the right trade already made for you.

## Sound is information, not atmosphere

Engine noise tells you what class of vehicle is near and roughly where. Shell impacts tell you
whether you penetrated. Aircraft are usually audible before they are visible.

Use headphones, and do not run the game at a volume where you stop noticing the difference between a
light tank's engine and a heavy's. If you play with music or a stream in the background, this is the
advantage you are giving up — and it is a large one.

## Controls: bind the things nobody binds

The default bindings cover driving and shooting. The ones that decide matches are usually unbound or
buried:

- **Scouting** (light tanks and some mediums) — marks an enemy for your entire team. It is free
  Research Points and free intelligence, and a large fraction of players never press it.
- **Ammunition selection.** Switching between armour-piercing and high-explosive should be a key you
  hit without thinking, not a menu you open under fire.
- **Binocular view and gunner sight**, on separate, comfortable keys. Ranging a shot means moving
  between them constantly.
- **Repair and crew replenishment**, so you can start a repair the instant you break a track.
- **Air: flaps, and manual engine controls** if you fly Realistic. Flaps alone change what turns you
  can survive.

Bindings are stored per vehicle class. Setting them up once for tanks does not set them for aircraft,
which is why so many players fly with defaults they never chose.

## Test Drive and Test Flight are free

Every vehicle can be taken into an empty test map at no cost. This is the correct place to learn what
a new tank's gun handling is like, where its own weak points are, and how it accelerates — rather
than discovering it in a match that charges you for the privilege. It costs nothing and almost nobody
uses it.

## Console and cross-platform notes

The game runs on PC, Linux, Mac, PlayStation and Xbox, with cross-platform play across them. Control
settings and graphics presets are per platform; the account and its research are not. If you play on
both a console and a PC, expect to do the binding work twice — and do it, because the defaults are
where the missing scouting key comes from.
