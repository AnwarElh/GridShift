Carjacking has worked the same way for most of the series' life. Approach a car, press one button,
remove the driver, drive off. If it was parked, the same button broke the window. *GTA 6* rebuilds
that from the ground up, and **GameSpot**'s breakdown of the extended look gathers what is
currently known. Everything below is from pre-release footage and Rockstar's own statements —
none of it is confirmed final.

## Two situations, and neither is one button

You are either taking a **stationary** car or one that **someone is driving**. They now play
differently.

For a parked car, Jason gets a menu of options rather than a prompt. One is the **slim jim** — the
strip of metal you slide down a window frame to work the lock — and in *GTA 6* using it appears to
open a **lockpicking minigame** rather than resolving instantly.

The slim jim does not work on everything. Whether it works appears to depend on the **year and
model** of the car, and newer, more expensive vehicles are expected to resist it.

For an occupied car, some are simply unlocked and you can open the door. From there it looks like a
**quick-time sequence** to wrestle the driver out. If there is a **passenger**, they will also try
to stop you.

## The tracker is the real change

This is the part that alters how the whole thing feels: cars have **trackers**, and police can
follow your position after you steal one.

In previous *GTA* games a stolen car was a small, local problem — a nearby officer might notice,
and losing them was usually straightforward. Now the vehicle itself reports you. Getting away
cleanly requires an **item that disables the tracking device**, and per GameSpot neither that item
nor the slim jim is something you start the game holding. Both come from **story progress or side
objectives**.

There is one reported wrinkle worth knowing: if you drive **fast enough**, the police reportedly
cannot track you. That inverts the usual instinct. Slowing down to look innocent is what gets you
caught, and the safe play is to keep moving.

Stealing an occupied car does not exempt you either. The tracker is still in the vehicle, and you
still have to deal with it.

## Know before you steal: the Waink app

There is an in-game app called **Waink** that scans a vehicle and reports what security systems it
carries, along with what it would cost to **register it as your own**.

That turns carjacking into something you can plan. Instead of grabbing whatever is at the kerb, you
can check whether a given car is slim-jim-able, whether it is going to broadcast your position, and
whether it is worth keeping at all.

## What is still unknown

Rockstar has not detailed how the lockpicking minigame is scored, whether failure raises attention,
how the tracker-disabling item is acquired specifically, or how much registering a vehicle costs.
Treat the above as the shape of the system rather than its final numbers.

*GTA 6* launches **November 19** on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S.
